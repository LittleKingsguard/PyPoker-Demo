## 1. The pass, and the sample it replays

A run keeps one decision stream and it is the pass. The loop consumes a hand's batch and discards it (§3), so after a variant's training at a seed ends the run plays that variant's **greedy pass** — at the run's own seed, on the run's own shape, against the same frozen pool — and records it under the run record's `greedy` block (`sizing.md` §4, `records.md` §15, ASM-38, D-29):

    _sample(budget)                                       -> min(int(budget), HANDS_BOUND)
    _pass(pool, shape, seed, hands, policy, where)        -> {mode, seed, hands, net_chips, decisions}

`_pass` returns those five keys and no sixth, one block per variant per seed, keyed in the record exactly as `curves` is (`record.md` §1, `docs/L2/training/surface.md` §4). It plays the `hands` it is **asked** for; `_sample` is the one rule that decides how many, and `loop` asks for `_sample(budget)` and never for the budget (§4).

**The sample is the run's own prefix.** One pass plays `S = _sample(budget)` hands: `S = budget` when the budget is at most 2,000, and exactly 2,000 when it is above. Hand *j* of the pass, for `j = 0 … S − 1`, **is the run's own hand *j***: `episode._hand(pool, shape, seed, j, policy)` derives the deal seed and the opponent draw seed from `(seed, j)`, takes the learner's seat as `_seat(j, shape)` — `j mod seats`, over the seat count `engine.game_config` declares — and fills every other seat with that hand's opponents (`episode.md` §1, `seeds.md` §2, `seats.md` §4, ASM-2, ASM-43). So the sample is the run's **prefix of length S**, and it is stated exactly by the record's own `budget`, the block's `hands` and the block's `seed` together with the rule `S = min(budget, 2,000)`: a third party replays it hand for hand, and no field beyond the five the block carries is needed to say which hands it was (§4, §5). Because the loop and the pass call that one function, the pass's seat split and its opponent membership are the **prefix's**: at or below the bound the split is the run's own, and above it it is the first 2,000 hands' — 334/334/333/333/333/333 over 2,000 hands on a six-seat shape, proportional to a 20,000-hand run's rather than identical to it (`sizing.md` §4, `ladder.md` §6, `evaluation/pool.md` §4, `seats.md` §4, ASM-16, ASM-43).

**And the identity is measured where it can be, and structural where it cannot.** At or below the bound hand *j* of the pass is measured to be the run's hand *j*: field for field against a pre-change 1,000-hand capture by `tests/integration/training/test_greedy_sample.py::TRNJ-13.1`, and hand for hand by `tests/training/test_greedy.py::TRN-14.8`, which builds a small pass from the checkpoint the record names (`evaluation.policy_from(final, "off")`) and holds its hands to **the same run's** `greedy` block prefix. Above the bound the identity is **structural** rather than measured, because no run's block is long enough to compare against: `_pass` deals `episode._hand(pool, shape, seed, index)` for `index` in `range(hands)`, with no budget in scope, so a 2,000-hand pass is the run's first 2,000 deals by construction and TRN-14.8's hand-independence case — the pass's hand *j* does not depend on how many hands were asked for — is the necessary condition beside it (`episode.md` §1, `seeds.md` §2, `records.md` §15, D-36).

**Below the bound the rule is the identity.** At or under 2,000 hands `S = budget`, so the pass replays exactly the sample such a run always replayed: a ten-hand run yields a ten-hand pass and a live-budget run a 1,000-hand one, and such a run's block is identical, field for field, to the block it produced before this rule existed — the record's `timings` being the one field two runs of one tuple differ in (§5) — which is what `tests/integration/training/test_greedy_sample.py` measures against a pre-change 1,000-hand capture (`sizing.md` §4, `shapes.md` §5, D-36).

**Both sides play the run's shape, and its seating is the shape's.** On a two-seat shape the hand is `engine.play_hand(hand_seed, seat0, seat1, shape=shape)`, the two positional policies swapped so the learner holds `_seat(i, shape)`; on a shape with more seats it is `engine.play_hand(hand_seed, seats=(...), shape=shape)`, the learner's policy inserted at that seat and `seats − 1` distinct members drawn in one `evaluation.opponent(pool, draw_seed, seats - 1)` call (`e2e-surface.md` §3.1, §3.4, `seats.md` §4, ASM-43). Either way the learner's six-index decisions are made and applied in the run's own vocabulary and every opponent meets them on the same game — never on the engine's default — so a six-seat run's block is the same sample training played, at six seats and 600 chips in play (`engine/surface.md` §10, `shapes.md` §2, §8, D-29, D-33).

**The mode is the policy's, and it is `off`.** `MODE = "off"` is the one mode a retained decision carries: the decision is `agents.as_policy`'s policy at `exploration="off"` — the masked maximum and `rule.md` §5's tie-break alone, with **no generator drawn from** — so two passes over one model decide identically and the block is a function of the model, the hands and the pool and not of a seed (`docs/L3/agents/rule.md` §4, §7, D-19). A block whose `mode` is anything else, or a census computed over training decisions, is refused by name rather than read (`sizing.md` §7).

**It writes nothing, and it reads the model the record names.** The pass is played after `agents.save` of the pair's final model (`loop.md` §1 step 8), so the block is reproducible from the checkpoint the record's `checkpoints` block points at, and the pass itself adds no artefact, no path and no file (`checkpoints.md` §3, `records.md` §15).

## 2. The block, key by key, and the decision record

`records.md` §15's five block keys, and where each number comes from:

| Key | Content |
|---|---|
| `mode` | `off`, `MODE`'s one value — the policy's mode and never the schedule's (`sizing.md` §4, D-28) |
| `seed` | the run seed the pass was played at, which is the seed the record's `seeds` list carries (`records.md` §15, ASM-2) |
| `hands` | the hands played: the sample's count, `min(budget, 2,000)` — the run's budget at or below the bound and exactly 2,000 above it — and never more than 2,000 (§4). It is **not** the run's budget above the bound, and no sixth key states the sample: the record's `budget` with this `hands` and the block's `seed` and §4's rule are the whole statement of it (`sizing.md` §4, D-36) |
| `net_chips` | one net chip delta per hand for the learner's seat, so the sample's price can be recomputed and not only quoted (`records.md` §5, §15, ASM-23) |
| `decisions` | the ordered decision records of the learner's own seat, at most `declarations.greedy_decisions(shape)` — 1,000 × the run's shape's history capacity: 24,000 at the default shape, 66,000 and 98,000 on the uncapped ones (§4, `shapes.md` §8, ASM-42) |

`_net_chips(payoffs, seat)` reads the hand's zero-sum outcome at the learner's seat, accepts the seat index the readout keys it by, and recomputes nothing: the pass reports the sample's price, averages nothing and thresholds nothing (`records.md` §5, §15, ASM-23, D-24). A six-seat hand's `payoffs` hold one net delta per seat and the block records the learner's own, so `net_chips` is the seat's price and not the hand's (`seats.md` §3, ASM-43).

`_decision_record(decision, shape)` returns §15's seven keys and no eighth, read off the episode decision the hand resolved (`records.md` §5, §15):

| Key | Content |
|---|---|
| `index`, `descriptor` | the chosen index, and the descriptor it decodes to through `features.decode_action(index, shape=shape)` — **the run's shape's** declared order, and never the default shape's three (`features/surface.md` §9, `sizing.md` §3) |
| `amount`, `minimum`, `maximum` | the raise-to the engine resolved and the two bounds it was resolved between, all three `None` on a decision where no raise was legal (`sizing.md` §2, `records.md` §5) |
| `legal` | the decision's legal index set through `features.legal_indices`, so a census re-derives no mask (`sizing.md` §7, `features/surface.md` §8) |
| `tie` | `false` on every decision — the one key the exploration-off surface does not report, and §6 says exactly what that is worth (`sizing.md` §3, V-F13) |

The index is the applied descriptor's position in that decision's own `legal` list (`episode._index`, `features/surface.md` §8), so the ordered log and the mask beside it are one vocabulary's: every retained index is a member of its own `legal` set and one of the shape's declared indices (`sizing.md` §3).

## 3. Why nothing else is retained (D-28)

A log of the behaviour policy would be a log of the **exploration schedule**: at the live budget almost the whole sample sits on the ε ramp, so every action class clears a floor by construction and a census of it cannot tell a learner that found a band from one that learned nothing — the defect D-28 records as fatal (`sizing.md` §4, `docs/L3/agents/rule.md` §6, ASM-38). So training decisions are not retained: the loop keeps its batch aggregate, the hand's `td_error`, and the curve, and nothing per decision (`curves.md` §3, D-28).

The record tree therefore holds an ordered decision log in **exactly one** place, and it is `greedy` (`records.md` §12, §15, ASM-38). A second log anywhere in the record is the defect D-28 named, not an addition to it.

**What a run retains instead is a function of what it already fixed.** The block is read off the finished model, over the run's own hands and the frozen pool, with no generator drawn from — so it inherits the tuple's determinism instead of adding a stream of its own, and §5 states that guarantee together with the one caveat it carries.

## 4. The sample rule, the two bounds and their refusals

The hand bound is `declarations.GREEDY_HANDS = 2000` **sampled hands** per variant per seed — the count one pass may play, not the count a run may train — and the record bound is **the run's own shape's**: `declarations.GREEDY_DECISIONS_PER_SLOT = 1000` records per slot of that shape's history capacity, read through `declarations.greedy_decisions(shape) = 1000 × declarations.shape_history(shape)` — 24,000 at the default shape, 66,000 and 98,000 on the uncapped ones (`sizing.md` §4, `records.md` §15, `shapes.md` §8, ASM-38, ASM-42). `greedy.HANDS_BOUND` reads the hand bound and `greedy.DECISIONS_BOUND` names the **default shape's** 24,000, so a reader who holds no shape has the shipped number in one place; the check reads `greedy_decisions`, so the check and the refusal cannot drift apart on any shape (`declarations.py`, `shapes.md` §8, D-33).

**The rule that fixes the sample, and the refusals that stay.** `_sample(budget)` is `min(int(budget), HANDS_BOUND)` and reads the module's own `HANDS_BOUND`, never a `2000` written down a second time: `declarations.GREEDY_HANDS` is the one declaration and every layer above this one quotes the rule in its terms (`declarations.py` §2, `docs/L2/training/surface.md` §4, D-36). `loop` hands `_pass` `_sample(budget)`; `_pass` hands `_check_hands` the count it was asked for.

- **`_check_hands(hands, where)` — the hand bound, on the pass's own request.** `_pass` calls it on the number of hands it is asked to play, so a **pass request** of 2,001 or 200,000 hands is refused naming the count it asked for and the 2,000-hand bound (`errors.md` §4). **A run is never refused for its budget**: no bound is checked at resolve, and the per-pair step (`_train_variant_seed`, §1 step 8) asks for `_sample(budget)`, so the shipped 200,000-hand budget trains and passes over its first 2,000 hands instead of failing before its first one (`loop.md` §1–§2, `sizing.md` §4, `e2e-surface.md` §2, ASM-38, D-36).
- **`_check_decisions(recorded, where, shape=None)` — the record bound, refused as the pass accumulates.** The bound is read per call from `declarations.greedy_decisions(shape)`, so a pass on a shape with a wider history block is bounded by that block and not by the default shape's: a hand holds a handful of decisions, the shape's capacity bounds how many the learner can have made before its block overflowed, and `records.md` §15 makes the pass's bound 1,000 × that capacity — never guessed from the hand count (`sizing.md` §4, `records.md` §15, `shapes.md` §8, ASM-38, ASM-42). `shape` omitted is the default shape, which is the bound `tests/training/` drives.

Both raise `errors.GreedyPassRefused` and neither clamps anything: a truncated log silently reports a **different sample**, so the refusal is the record's protection and not an inconvenience (`records.md` §15, `sizing.md` §4, `errors.md` §2). The hand refusal names **the count the pass was asked for and the 2,000-hand bound**; the record refusal names **the count the pass would reach, the bound, the shape and that shape's history capacity**, so a reader learns which shape's bound was crossed (`errors.md` §4, `declarations.greedy_decisions`, ASM-42). **A pass of exactly a bound is inside it**: 2,000 hands and 24,000 records are legal at the default shape, and 2,001 hands, 24,001 records, 66,001 on `no-limit-100` and 98,001 on `six-seat-no-limit-100` are refused (`shapes.md` §8, ASM-42, D-33).

**A request that is not a positive integer is refused, and no block contradicts itself.** `_check_hands` refuses a request that is a `bool`, a float, a string, 0 or a negative, naming the request as given and the contract it breaks — a pass plays **at least one** hand and at most `HANDS_BOUND` — before `_pass` deals its first hand, so `int()` never truncates a float request and no block is ever returned whose `hands` is not a positive integer equal to `len(net_chips)` (`errors.md` §4, D-36). `_sample` is fed the run's own budget, which `run` validates as a positive integer of at least ten hands (`InvalidBudget`), so the rule's only inputs are the request and the two bounds (`loop.md` §6, D-36).

**The two bounds are enforced at two distances.** The hand bound is a **private-contract seam** — `_check_hands` through `_pass`, contract for `tests/training/` and not a caller-facing refusal, because no run asks the pass for a count the bound refuses — while the record bound **is** caller-facing inside a run: `_check_decisions` is raised while the run's own pass is being played, so a pass that would cross it fails that run by name (`errors.md` §4, `README.md` §4, `loop.md` §1 step 8, D-36).

**Why the prefix, and what it rejects.** Four requirements fix the form — it must **reduce to the identity below the bound** (a run at or under 2,000 hands must reproduce its pre-change block), be **replayable from the record's tuple** (`budget`, `hands` and `seed` plus this rule, and nothing else), add **no decision stream** (the pass draws from no generator, §1), and **nest** (a budget sweep must not move the evaluation sample, §5) — and each rejected alternative fails at least one of them. D-36 phrases the ruling as a sample "drawn from the run's own distribution"; the prefix is that ruling's form here, since the hands are the run's own and nothing is drawn. So:

- **Rejected: a stride or decimation** — hand *j* of the pass = run hand `floor(j·budget/S)`. It fails the first requirement above the bound and it aliases with the seat rotation: at a 20,000-hand budget with `S = 2,000` the stride is 10, so on a two-seat shape every sampled index is even and the pass would record **one seat only**, misreporting the seat split the record states — and at six seats a stride of 10 reaches three seats of six. It also buys nothing: spreading an i.i.d. draw changes the variance of a statistic while the pass reads **one fixed final model**, so spacing moves variance and not bias.
- **Rejected: a fresh generator or hash draw of a subset.** It fails the second and third requirements: it adds a decision stream the design says a run does not have (§1), and the sample would no longer be replayable from the record's tuple.
- **Rejected: the tail** (the run's last `S` hands). Valid on the first three, and it fails **nesting**: with a prefix, a 10,000-hand and a 20,000-hand run at one seed pass over the *same* first 2,000 hands, so a budget sweep's difference is attributable to training and not to a different evaluation sample; a tail's sample moves whenever only the budget moves.
- **Rejected: raising the cap.** That is the defect D-36 records: a 200,000-hand pass would carry ~200,000 chip deltas and hundreds of thousands of decision records, which is exactly what the two bounds exist to prevent (`sizing.md` §4, `records.md` §15, ASM-38).

**What this costs, stated plainly.** Above the bound the pass reports a sample of the run's hands and not all of them, so `net_chips` is a 2,000-hand estimate of the sample's price rather than a budget-long one, and a reader who wants to know which share of the run the block covers reads it off the record: `budget` is the hands the run trained and `hands` the hands the pass replayed, so the share is `hands / budget` (`records.md` §12, §15, D-36). Below the bound the two are one number and the record is what it always was.

## 5. What reproduces the block

Two runs of one `(configuration, seed set, budget, pool membership)` **in one process** produce an identical `greedy` block — the same seed, hands, chip deltas and ordered decisions — because the pass's hands, seats and opponents are derivations of `(seed, j)` and of the run's shape, because it reads the finished model whose training that tuple fixed, and because `exploration="off"` draws from no generator (`seeds.md` §2, `episode.md` §1, `docs/L3/agents/rule.md` §4, §7, ASM-2, ASM-38). The seating is the shape's, so the rotation is too: a six-seat block rotates the learner through six seats in the order `_seat` names, and the seat each hand was played in is a function of the index and of the shape and of nothing else (`seats.md` §4, ASM-43, D-33). The record's `timings` stay the one field two such runs differ in (`docs/L2/training/surface.md` §4, `loop.md` §7).

**And the sample nests, which is what a budget sweep rests on.** Because the pass takes the run's **first** `min(budget, 2,000)` hands, two runs at one seed whose budgets both exceed the bound pass over the *same* 2,000 hands: they differ in their blocks only through the model training produced, and never through a different evaluation sample. Across the bound the sample stops at 2,000 while the run keeps training, so the pass's hand count is `_sample(budget)`'s consequence and no second rule a reader must combine with it (`sizing.md` §4, D-36).

**The guarantee is exactly the one the record already has, and no wider.** The whole training path is hash-order sensitive across processes — a pre-existing property `/tmp/pypoker-baseline/verify.py`'s `UNSTABLE_NUMERICS` records — so this document promises one block per tuple **within a process**, which is the guarantee `docs/L2/training/surface.md` §4 states, and promises nothing about two processes (`loop.md` §7). The pinned reproduction is **measured**, not asserted: `tests/integration/training/test_greedy_sample.py::TRNJ-13.1` replays a 1,000-hand run in a child pinned to `PYTHONHASHSEED=0` — the pin that capture was taken under — and holds that child's whole summary to the capture field for field (`battery.md` §5, `guardrails.md` G20, D-36).

**And the stream a run leaves.** The pass replays the sample rather than extending it: every hand it deals is a hand training already dealt, at that hand's own deal seed, so no deal beyond the sample is made and `engine.play_hand` leaves the global `random` stream where that deal left it. **At or below the bound** its last hand is the one training's last hand was dealt at, so a run that passes leaves the stream exactly where its training left it and a later run in the same process is not perturbed by it — which is what `tests/integration/training/test_run_record.py::TRNJ-8.3` measures. **Above the bound** the pass stops at hand `S − 1` and the stream stands there, which the run's own record does not depend on: nothing of this module seeds or advances the stream, and a second run at the same tuple still reproduces the first because every hand is dealt at its own derived seed (`seeds.md` §2, `engine/surface.md` §4, ASM-2, D-36).

## 6. The `tie` key — the one thing this module cannot observe

`records.md` §15 makes `tie` "whether the tie-break, and not a strict maximum, chose the index" (`sizing.md` §3, validity V-F13). **No granted surface reports that flag for an exploration-off decision**, and this is a named limitation rather than a reading of the sample:

- `agents.as_policy` returns a **policy** — a callable from `(readout, legal)` to one descriptor and nothing else — and the flag its rule computes is discarded before the descriptor is returned;
- `evaluation.policy_from`'s policy is that same callable with that same discard, so the other exploration-off surface does not report it either;
- `agents.act`'s decision record, the one record in the tree that carries the flag, carries it on the **schedule** arm, whose decision is not this pass's (`e2e-surface.md` §3.3–§3.4, `docs/L3/agents/rule.md` §5, `agents/surface.md` §6, D-19).

So `_decision_record` writes `tie: False` for every decision, and the block claims exactly what that is worth: **no tie-resolved decision is reported**. That is not the claim that none occurred — the tie-break decides wherever two masked values stand within `rule.md` §5's `1e-9` relative tolerance of the masked maximum — so a reader must not treat the block's `tie` column as evidence of a strict maximum, and a census must not divide by it.

**The census's tie-excluded conjunct is therefore not computable from this block.** `sizing.md` §7's conjunct 7 re-runs conjuncts 3–6 with `T(s)`, the seed's tie-resolved decision count, excluded; with no reported flag `T(s)` reads as 0 from this artefact, which makes the conjunct either vacuous or a claim about a number this module never measured. That gap is an `ADJUDICATION` and it is returned to L1, on `modules.md` §3 / D-13's route: this module's granted interface cannot meet a claim its record makes, which is an interface defect answered above this layer and never repaired here by inventing a field, a seventh entry point or an inference (D-12, D-13, `module-map.md` §2).

**What would close it, and whose it is.** The flag must reach the policy protocol: `agents.as_policy` — and `evaluation.policy_from` with it — returning the tie flag beside the descriptor, one word wider and no new name in this module (`e2e-surface.md` §3.3–§3.4, `agents/surface.md` §6). Until that lands, a census that excludes tie-resolved decisions must read them from somewhere other than this block, and `sizing.md` §7's conjunct 7 is L1's to hold, re-price or withhold.

## 7. What is left to L4

Whether the pass accumulates its decision records in a list or a generator the block copies, how `where` is spelled, whether the two bounds are read at the call or once at import, and every private name other than `MODE`, `HANDS_BOUND`, `DECISIONS_BOUND`, `_sample`, `_check_hands`, `_check_decisions`, `_pass`, `_decision_record` and `_net_chips`, whose spellings and signatures are contract for `tests/training/` (`README.md` §4) — `_sample`'s one argument and its `int` result, `_pass`'s six arguments with the fourth being the hands asked for, and `_check_decisions`' `shape` argument among them. The **placement** is not among them: `loop.md` §1 step 8 fixes that the per-pair step asks `_pass` for `_sample(budget)`.

Not L4's: the sample — the run's own **first `min(budget, 2,000)` hands**, its shape's rotation and its opponents, seated by `episode._hand` — the rule itself being `min(budget, GREEDY_HANDS)` and read off `HANDS_BOUND` rather than a literal, a run never being refused for its budget while a pass **request** above the bound still is, that a request which is not a positive integer is refused before a hand is dealt, the mode being `off` and the policy's, the five block keys and the seven decision keys, `tie` being `False` with §6's statement of what that means, the two bounds with the record bound the run's own shape's and their refusals with their counts, the pass writing nothing and coming after the save, the prefix's four rejected alternatives and the reason each fails, and the reproduction and nesting claims of §5 with their caveats (`guardrails.md` G21–G23, G27, `seats.md` §4–§5, `shapes.md` §8, ASM-38, ASM-42, ASM-43, D-28, D-33, D-36).
