# PyPoker-Demo — L1 assumptions

- **Layer:** L1 · **Unit:** phase1 · **Rev:** 7 · **Date:** 2026-11-07
- **Purpose:** the decisions L1 made where the brief left the answer to this layer, each with the alternative it rejected
- **Ceiling:** 90 lines. At the ceiling, split by owner — never append

ASM-1 to ASM-5 and ASM-13 are the brief's own open assumptions and are not restated here. ASM-3
delegates the learning algorithm to L1 and its answer is ASM-7. ASM-13 asks L1 for the pool size, the
hands per pairing and the match count, and its answers are ASM-14 and ASM-15. ASM-6 and ASM-7..ASM-12
are Rev 1's and are kept; ASM-14..ASM-19 are Rev 2's, forced by D-16. Each is a candidate for
ratification into the brief's §7 by L0. At Rev 3, ASM-15 keeps its id and its subject but states the
cost the brief's **ASM-21** ratified from measurement, because ASM-21 supersedes the 40 ms arithmetic
ASM-15 was first written with; the id is kept so every citation to it still resolves (ASM-21). At
Rev 5, ASM-15 quotes ASM-21's amended band and its projections, adding only the band's slow-end
arithmetic, so no layer below this one has an older number to copy. Rev 6 writes ASM-20, which three
downstream documents and the module itself already cited and no layer had defined. Rev 7 removes the per-build
seconds ASM-15 had grown: the assumption now fixes the **band and the shape** and sends a reader to the artefact for
the current values, because walkthrough run 4 found four documents quoting a superseded build's figures
(`strata/walkthrough.md` §5).

| Id | Assumption | Alternative rejected |
|---|---|---|
| ASM-6 | The fixed game is parameterised as two seats, blinds 1/2 with one blind level that never rises, starting stacks 20 chips, fixed bets of 2 and 4, and a raise cap of 4 per street — inside ASM-1's short-stack direction, and small enough that a hand is a handful of decisions (D-2). The small bet of 2 is the preflop and flop bet and the big bet of 4 the turn and river bet, and the cap is four bets on a street — the opening bet plus three raises (D-2) | Deep stacks of about 100 chips with an uncapped number of raises: it multiplies the per-hand decision count for no gain in what Phase 1 compares, and it multiplies the pure-Python engine cost that C-C2 flags and ASM-15 bounds |
| ASM-7 | **The answer to ASM-3.** One learning rule for the whole ladder: masked Q-learning over the three-index action vocabulary, with the reward, exploration schedule, optimiser family, batch size and target-update interval held fixed. The function approximator is hand-rolled in NumPy at R1 and in PyTorch from R2 on (D-7), so R1 and R2 are the same model on the same work and R3–R6 change the representation or the architecture only (D-3) | ASM-3's suggested tabular R1: a table cannot carry "a learned card vector", so R3 would have to introduce both an embedding table and a function approximator in one rung — two changes at once, which D-3 forbids and V-F7 warns about. Also rejected: a policy-gradient method from R2 on, which changes the learning rule on the rung where the network first appears |
| ASM-8 | **Revised at Rev 2.** Budgets are matched in **samples**, not wall-clock: 1,000 hands per variant per seed live and 200,000 for the shipped runs, and the comparison is measured at one table budget — 120 hands per cell live, 1,500 full — at the three fixed seeds of `protocol.md` §2 (D-6, D-8, D-16) | A wall-clock-matched protocol: each rung would then train on a different number of hands, so the comparison would no longer be sample-matched and a slower rung would be reported as worse for being slow (D-6, C-C13) |
| ASM-9 | R4, R5 and R6 each declare a **pair** of variants at one budget, and the pair is the rung's own control — wide against deep, dense against sparse, residual against plain (D-3, D-6) | One configuration per rung: then "width vs depth" is never compared, and a rung would be reported on a curve alone, which D-6 forbids and C-C8 names as naming a technique instead of demonstrating one |
| ASM-10 | A comparison pair is matched to 10,000 trainable parameters within ±2%, measured by `agents.param_count`, so width, depth, density and the residual path are varied at one capacity (D-3, C-C13) | Leaving each variant to pick its own size: width, depth, density and residual would then be confounded with capacity, and the axis the rung is named for could not be read from the result |
| ASM-11 | Path ownership is a declaration, not authorship: `pyproject.toml`, the lock file, `tests/conftest.py` and `tests/e2e/` are claimed by `harness`, and the shipped tables by `evaluation` under `tables/`, so no changed path escapes ownership, while the e2e battery is authored at L1 and the diff-scope check runs over paths inside module subtrees (modules.md §4, D-13) | Leaving root packaging, the battery or the shipped table unclaimed: a scaffolding or table change would then fail the diff-scope check as "owned by no module", and the only alternatives are a lint exemption or a module that exists to own files |
| ASM-12 | The reward is the seat's net chip delta for the completed hand, delivered at the terminal decision, with no shaping and no intermediate term — the zero-sum payoff of `engine.payoffs` (D-6) | Shaped per-decision rewards: a shaped reward is a second thing that would vary between rungs, and it biases the comparison the ladder exists to make (D-3, D-6) |
| ASM-14 | **The answer to ASM-13 on size.** The pool keeps M = 5 versions plus the three pinned baselines; a table's membership is that pool plus the rung's variants, so K is 4 for R1, 9 or 10 once the window is full, and the table has K(K−1)/2 cells — 45 at worst. A cell is 120 hands live and 1,500 full, seats alternating, at one hand count for every pair (D-16) | A larger M, or larger cells, at the same cost: M = 20 or 2,000-hand cells would put the live table past the minutes D-8 allows, and a table of a few dozen hands per cell would report rank differences that are noise. Also rejected: a separate, smaller membership for the live table, which would make the live and full tables two designs and the live one unreadable against the full one (D-16, D-8) |
| ASM-15 | **The answer to ASM-13 on cost, amended to the brief's ASM-21.** The per-hand cost is a **measurement, not a bound**, so this assumption fixes its **band and its shape and no figure of its own**: **0.105–0.153 s** of CPU per hand across the three measured artefacts (6.5–9.5 hands/s) — ASM-21's 0.105–0.120 for a live run, 0.1299–0.1324 for the shipped full table and 0.1415–0.1532 for the live table — because the oracle's equity sample is nearly all of a table's cost and a K = 4 table has it in half its hands while the run faces it in one hand in three (an oracle-bearing cell measured 0.255–0.315 s/hand against 0.002–0.004 for an oracle-free one). Each artefact records its own seconds in its own shape — a run's `timings`, the full table's `cost.seconds_per_hand`, the live table's `stages.matrix_live` over 720 hands — and **the values are read from the artefact** (`checkpoints/evidence/r1-live.json`, `tables/league-full.json`) **and never from a document**, because a regeneration moves every second-count and leaves only the band (`protocol.md` §2, `strata/walkthrough.md` §7). At the measured band the live table is ≈95–110 s at K = 4 and ≈10–12 minutes at K = 9–10 (4,320–5,400 hands), and the full table ≈2 hours (2.5 h at K = 10 at the shipped rate); holding the band's slow end, 0.1532 s/hand, instead is the conservative bound — ≈14 minutes at K = 10 live and ≈2.3–2.9 hours full, an over-statement because a wider table pairs the oracle in a smaller share of its hands — the difference between a talk and an offline job that D-8 asks for. `harness.timings` reports the real figure and FAM-10 asserts the record, never a wall clock (ASM-21, ASM-14, D-16, D-8) | Keeping the earlier stated bound of 40 ms of CPU per hand operative (at most 24 decisions at about 1.5 ms per decision): the arithmetic did not price the oracle's equity query at every decision, so it was 3.2× optimistic and would report a ten-minute table as under four minutes (ASM-21). Also rejected: promoting one build's measured seconds to this assumption's figure, which the next regeneration invalidates — the drift walkthrough run 4 found in four documents (`strata/walkthrough.md` §5). And rejected: deriving the cell size from a wall-clock target, which would make the table's resolution depend on the machine the talk runs on, and buying cost back with concurrency, which one global `random` stream forbids (D-16, ASM-2) |
| ASM-16 | **The frozen-pool reading of D-16.** A rung's pool is the last M versions of the rung it inherits plus the baselines, frozen before either arm trains; both arms of a pair therefore sample one pool and record one pool id, and a rung's own snapshots become the next rung's window (D-16, D-3) | A within-run rolling pool in which each arm's own snapshots enter its own training pool: the two arms would then train against different opponents and different data distributions, so D-16's "identical frozen pool" would be false and a pair's difference would no longer be one axis (D-3, D-16) |
| ASM-17 | **The rating.** Bradley-Terry fitted on the cells' win/tie/loss counts with ties halved, a 95% interval from a seeded hand-level bootstrap, anchored so the three baselines' mean rating is 0, with a bounded rating for a member that won or lost everything; the raw matrix is reported alongside because the fit assumes a transitivity poker payoffs need not have (D-16) | Elo: an online update is order-dependent and needs a k-factor chosen at L1, so the rating would carry a schedule artefact the matrix does not have (D-16). Also rejected: a rating over mean chip delta rather than hand outcomes, which is not a win probability and would let one large pot outvote many hands (ASM-12, D-16) |
| ASM-18 | **The snapshots.** A run writes ten equally spaced intra-run snapshots plus its final model, and the pool's window retains the last M of them; `training.checkpoints` indexes exactly the pool plus each variant's final model (D-16, ASM-5) | Emitting exactly M snapshots: the window would never roll, so "bounded rolling" would be a word for a fixed set (D-16). Also rejected: a snapshot per update, which makes checkpoint churn dominate a live run's wall clock (D-8, C-C2) |
| ASM-19 | **The league's owner.** The pool, the scheduler, the matrix and the rating are `evaluation`'s, so one object is both the training opposition and the evaluation opposition; the map stays at six modules (D-16, D-13) | A seventh `league` module: it would own no data `evaluation` does not, split the pool's membership from its meaning across two owners, add a hop in front of `evaluation`'s own match loop, and give one more agent a claim on the same checkpoints (D-16, D-13) |
| ASM-20 | **A checkpoint records the seed and the encoding it was built at, so a member is loadable from its path alone.** The container carries both facts and the reading of them is the policy builder's: `evaluation.policy_from(path)` takes a path and no seed, reads exactly those two facts from the file — what `agents`' restore is required to recover from the file alone and its surface declines to hand back — and hands the recorded seed to `agents.as_policy` over `agents.load(None, path)`, while the learner, its parameters, its rule and its format version stay `agents.load`'s alone; a file that records no seed or no encoding name, or records one in a form this module does not read, is refused by name and **no seed is ever substituted, a path digest included** (D-16, ASM-2, ASM-5, ASM-18, `records.md` §7, `protocol.md` §5). Written at Rev 6 to resolve a citation three documents and the module itself already made (`docs/L2/evaluation/surface.md` §12, `docs/L2/evaluation/league.md` §7, `docs/L3/evaluation/scheduler.md` §5.1) | A seed beside the path in the builder's signature — a `policy_from(path, seed=…)` — or a seed derived from the path: either is a second, unchecked claim about which learner the file holds, and a table could then replay a member at a seed the member's own record contradicts. Also rejected: having `agents` hand the two facts back through its surface so the caller need not read the file, which would put one module's reader inside another's restore and expose a learner's internals on the granted arrow (D-13, `agents/surface.md` §10) |

## What was not decided here

Nothing in this map needs the user: the algorithm was delegated to L1 by ASM-3, the league's sizes were
delegated by ASM-13, and the choice between Elo and Bradley-Terry was offered as a choice by D-16. The
rung order, the game, the three baselines and the existence of the league are taken as given (D-2, D-3,
D-6, D-16) and are not re-litigated.
