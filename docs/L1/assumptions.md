# PyPoker-Demo — L1 assumptions

- **Layer:** L1 · **Unit:** phase1 · **Rev:** 12 · **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Purpose:** the decisions L1 made where the brief left the answer to this layer, each with the alternative it rejected
- **Ceiling:** 90 lines. At the ceiling, split by owner — never append

ASM-1 to ASM-5 and ASM-13 are the brief's own open assumptions and are not restated here. ASM-3
delegates the learning algorithm to L1 and its answer is ASM-7. ASM-13 asks L1 for the pool size, the
hands per pairing and the match count, and its answers are ASM-14 and ASM-15. ASM-6 and ASM-7..ASM-12
are Rev 1's and are kept; ASM-14..ASM-19 are Rev 2's, forced by D-16. Each is a candidate for
ratification into the brief's §7 by L0. At Rev 3, ASM-15 keeps its id and its subject but states the
cost the brief's **ASM-21** ratified from measurement, because ASM-21 supersedes the 40 ms arithmetic
ASM-15 was first written with; the id is kept so every citation to it still resolves (ASM-21). At
Rev 5, ASM-15 quotes ASM-21's amended band and its projections, adding only the band's slow-end
arithmetic, so no layer below this one has an older number to copy. Rev 6 writes ASM-20, which three
downstream documents and the module itself already cited and no layer had defined. Rev 7 removes the per-build
seconds ASM-15 had grown: the assumption now fixes the **band and the shape** and sends a reader to the artefact for
the current values, because four documents quoted a superseded build's figures and this assumption is now the one
number that does not move (`strata/walkthrough.md` §5). Rev 8 wrote ASM-22, and Rev 9 rewrites it: a document cites
the **rule's carrier** — a `D-n`, an `ASM-n` or a document section — and never a finding, by subject or by number,
in `strata/records/phase1-walkthrough.md`, because that record is replaced on every run (D-14,
`strata/walkthrough.md` §7); ASM-18 still names the two artefact sets: the 33 paths a run **writes** for three seeds
and the 18 the repository **ships** (`protocol.md` §5). Rev 9 also corrects ASM-6's cap gloss and states the range
of the readout's raise count, which the surface takes as 0–4 on every street and the encodings now spell that way.
Rev 10 writes ASM-23, which extends the band-and-shape rule ASM-15 states for seconds to **every** figure a build
moves — a rating value, a cell's counts, a wall clock, a per-hand rate — and names the one place an exact figure
belongs (`protocol.md` §2). Rev 11 writes ASM-24, which settles what a split's redirect is for: the
engine's three splits left 25 citations naming a redirect instead of the rule's carrier, and the rule
that a citation names the carrier is now applied to them — the document sites by the layer that owns
each document, the sites in a `src/` or `tests/` comment, docstring or assertion message left to a code pass — with the redirects kept as
the safety net they were meant to be rather than as a way of addressing a rule (ASM-22,
`docs/L1/README.md`). Rev 12 answers D-19: ASM-7 keeps its id and its subject — the answer to ASM-3 —
and states that the rule is a **catalogue** whose members a spec selects from and that what a
comparison holds constant is the **set** of rules, not one rule; ASM-25 names the second rule and the
third that was declined, ASM-26 states the order, rolling and eviction of a pool that holds several
estimators, and ASM-27 states what the pool id certifies, which is the finding that the id digested
member ids alone and so could not certify an estimator label (D-19, `docs/L1/rules.md`). Rev 13 answers D-20,
which is the L0 decision ASM-25's rejected alternative said was needed rather than an L1 assumption: ASM-12
keeps its id and its subject and states that the net chip delta is the reward of the catalogue's **first**
utility rather than the reward of every model, ASM-27 keeps its id and its subject and adds the utility to
what the pool id digests, and ASM-28 and ASM-29 write the catalogue and the reading rule the table needs
(D-20, `docs/L1/utilities.md`).

| ASM-6 | The fixed game is parameterised as two seats, blinds 1/2 with one blind level that never rises, starting stacks 20 chips, fixed bets of 2 and 4, and a raise cap of 4 per street — inside ASM-1's short-stack direction, and small enough that a hand is a handful of decisions (D-2). The small bet of 2 is the preflop and flop bet and the big bet of 4 the turn and river bet. **The cap is four bet-or-raise actions on a street**, and it is not "the opening bet plus three raises" on every street: post-flop the four are the opening bet and three raises, while pre-flop the posted blind is a posting and not one of the four, so the blind is followed by **four raises — five bet levels in all** (`preflop seat 0 raises to 4`, `… seat 1 raises to 6`, `… seat 0 raises to 8`, `… seat 1 raises to 10` is a capped street, and at the cap `raise` leaves the legal set). `engine.readout`'s second `limit` entry counts those bet-or-raise actions, so it ranges **0–4 on every street** — not the 0–3 a raise-only reading gives — and the dense encoding's divisor for that slot is its own maximum, **4** (D-2, `e2e-surface.md` §2) | Deep stacks of about 100 chips with an uncapped number of raises: it multiplies the per-hand decision count for no gain in what Phase 1 compares, and it multiplies the pure-Python engine cost that C-C2 flags and ASM-15 prices by measurement in ASM-21's band |

| ASM-25 | **The second rule, and the third that was declined (D-19).** The catalogue holds exactly two members: `masked-q-learning`, which bootstraps from the masked maximum of the target copy's values, and `double-q-learning`, which lets the online parameters choose the index of the bootstrap and reads that index's value from the target copy — the decoupling of action selection from action evaluation that the diagnosis names as the remedy for an upward-biased maximum (D-19, `strata/records/r2-plateau-diagnosis.md`, `rules.md` §2). Both hold the same trainable parameters and the same state layout, so the pair is capacity-matched by its architecture alone (ASM-10). A **returns-based** third rule — Monte-Carlo or n-step, D-18's other named candidate — is declined: a return is defined over an episode and the transition record carries one decision and its successor with no episode boundary (`records.md` §4), so accepting any batch would make the rule learn nothing from a one-transition batch and refusing a batch without a terminal transition would give the catalogue members different **admissible inputs**, an interface difference D-19 forbids. No fourth is added (`rules.md` §5) | A returns-based third rule that refuses a terminal-less batch: the catalogue's members would then disagree about what `learn` accepts, so `training` would have to branch on the rule — the one thing D-19's shared contract exists to prevent. Also rejected: a third rule by *reward normalisation*, which changes the reward ASM-12 fixes as the unshaped net chip delta and so needs an L0 decision, not an L1 assumption |
| ASM-26 | **A pool that holds several estimators: its order, its rolling and its eviction (D-19).** The window's versions are grouped into **families**: a family is one lineage chain, every member of it declares one rule, and its label is the start its first member names. The window lists each family's members contiguously, oldest first, and the families in the order they entered it — for a declared membership, the order of each family's start in the declaration. `offer` extends exactly **one** family: the offered version's `lineage` must be the id of a member that is the newest of its family, or a label no member carries, which opens a new family; a version whose rule differs from the family it extends is refused, and so is a lineage that names a member which is not its family's newest, because the chain would fork. When the window already holds `m` versions, the one that leaves is **the oldest member of the family that currently holds the most members**, counting the version that has just joined, ties broken by which family's oldest member is the older — so no family is emptied while another holds two or more, and heterogeneity survives the bound: with F families resident, each keeps at least one member while F ≤ m, and an offer that would open the (m+1)th family is refused by name rather than emptying one. `build_pool` windows a too-rich declaration by the same rule and refuses a declaration holding more than `m` families (`league.md` §2, `docs/L3/evaluation/pool.md`) | **Oldest-first over the concatenated window**, which is the rule before D-19 and which can silently evict the last member of a family: the population would stop holding an estimator while the table still claimed to have fitted over it, and the run trained against a pool that no longer matched its own record (D-19, ASM-16). Also rejected: **one pool per rule**, which is two matrices and two ratings, and a rating cannot be fitted over two disjoint memberships with one anchor (D-16). Also rejected: **raising M** to hold every family's chain in full, which is D-16's and ASM-14's cost decision — cells rise as K(K−1)/2 — and not this assumption's |
| ASM-27 | **The pool id certifies each version's estimator and its utility, and a baselines-only pool keeps the id it had (D-19, restated at D-20).** The digest's payload is one line per member: a baseline contributes its **name** alone, and a version contributes `id`, the `\x1f` unit separator, then its `rule`, the separator again, then its `utility`. Two pools whose version ids are equal and whose rules or utilities are not therefore digest differently, which is what a table needs to say which population and which objectives a rating was fitted over; an id, a rule or a utility containing `\n`, `\r` or `\x1f` is refused, so no field can be smuggled across the separator; and `m`, `frozen` and every other member field stay outside the payload, so a corrected seed or step does not rename the pool (D-19, D-20, D-16, `records.md` §7, §8). A baselines-only pool digests exactly as before, which is why R1's pool id — the one id a shipped artefact records — survives this change without a regeneration (ASM-14, `protocol.md` §5) | Digesting the **ids alone**, which is the rule before D-19: a label could not be certified, so one member record could say `masked-q-learning` while its neighbour's said `double-q-learning` and the two memberships would wear one id. Also rejected: digesting the whole member record, which would rename a pool for a corrected `seed` or `step` and so break the rule that the id changes when and only when the membership does. Also rejected: prefixing the baselines' lines too, which would change every pool id in the tree — the shipped bundle's included — for no certification the version lines do not already give. Also rejected: leaving the utility out of the payload, which would repeat the D-19 finding for the objective — a pool that could not certify the utility its member was trained under |
| ASM-28 | **The utility catalogue (D-20).** The terminal reward becomes a per-variant **utility** selected exactly as the learning rule is (ASM-7): `agents.learners()["utilities"]` publishes **`net-chips`** — ASM-12's unshaped net chip delta, unchanged, and the declared default — and **`stack-shortfall`**, whose terminal reward is `(U(s_before) − U(s_after)) / U(s_0)` with `U(s) = (T − s)²`, `T` the total chips in play, `s_0` the stack a hand begins from and `s_before`/`s_after` the seat's stack before and after the outcome, delivered at the terminal decision only, with no intermediate term and no shaping. | ASM-29 | **The table keeps the game's currency, and a differently-motivated model is neither better nor worse by it (D-20).** Every cell stays in chips and hands — wins, ties, losses, mean chip delta per hand — because chips are what the game pays and the objective a model was trained under is not a defence against losing them; and because two models asked different questions are not like-for-like, every record and the report name the utility each member was trained under (`records.md` §7, §9, §10, §12, §13, `utilities.md` §7, §9). ## What was not decided here

Nothing in this map needs the user: the algorithm was delegated to L1 by ASM-3, the league's sizes were
delegated by ASM-13, and the choice between Elo and Bradley-Terry was offered as a choice by D-16. The
rung order, the game, the three baselines and the existence of the league are taken as given (D-2, D-3,
D-6, D-16) and are not re-litigated.

**What D-19 leaves to the user is its re-baseline, and L1 has not taken it.** Which rules the *ladder*
declares — R1–R6 each declaring the two-rule set rather than the one rule they declare today — is L0's
call, because it doubles every rung's trained variants, makes every rung's table K = 10, and re-measures
every shipped artefact; this layer builds the mechanism and proves it at the live budget, and states the
cost of that decision in its return rather than assuming it (D-19, D-8, ASM-8).

**What D-20 leaves to the user is the same call for the utility, and L1 has not taken it either.** The six
rungs declare `net-chips` for both arms, so no rung asks its two arms different questions and no shipped
artefact is re-measured; making a rung declare `stack-shortfall`, or adding a seventh rung whose axis is the
utility, is L0's, for the reason D-19's is: it changes what a rung's table measures and re-baselines the
evidence under it (D-20, D-8, `utilities.md` §10). What L1 has done is build the axis and prove it at the live
budget with two variants that differ only in utility (D-20, ASM-23).
