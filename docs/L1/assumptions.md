# PyPoker-Demo — L1 assumptions

- **Layer:** L1 · **Unit:** phase1 · **Rev:** 11 · **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Purpose:** the decisions L1 made where the brief left the answer to this layer, each with the alternative it rejected
- **Ceiling:** 90 lines. At the ceiling, split by owner — never append

ASM-1 to ASM-5 and ASM-13 are the brief's own open assumptions and are not restated here. ASM-3
delegates the learning algorithm to L1 and its answer is ASM-7. ASM-13 asks L1 for the pool size, the
hands per pairing and the match count, and its answers are ASM-14 and ASM-15. ASM-6 and ASM-7..ASM-12
are Rev 1's and are kept; ASM-14..ASM-19 are Rev 2's, forced by D-16. Each is a candidate for
ratification into the brief's §7 by L0. At Rev 3, ASM-15 keeps its id and its subject but states the
cost the brief's **ASM-21** ratified from measurement, because ASM-21 supersedes the 40 ms arithmetic
ASM-15 was first written with; the id is kept so every citation to it still resolves (ASM-21). At
Rev 5, ASM-15 quotes ASM-21's amended band and its projections, adding only the band's slow-end
arithmetic, so no layer below this one has an older number to copy. Rev 6 writes ASM-20, which three
downstream documents and the module itself already cited and no layer had defined. Rev 7 removes the per-build
seconds ASM-15 had grown: the assumption now fixes the **band and the shape** and sends a reader to the artefact for
the current values, because four documents quoted a superseded build's figures and this assumption is now the one
number that does not move (`strata/walkthrough.md` §5). Rev 8 wrote ASM-22, and Rev 9 rewrites it: a document cites
the **rule's carrier** — a `D-n`, an `ASM-n` or a document section — and never a finding, by subject or by number,
in `strata/records/phase1-walkthrough.md`, because that record is replaced on every run (D-14,
`strata/walkthrough.md` §7); ASM-18 still names the two artefact sets: the 33 paths a run **writes** for three seeds
and the 18 the repository **ships** (`protocol.md` §5). Rev 9 also corrects ASM-6's cap gloss and states the range
of the readout's raise count, which the surface takes as 0–4 on every street and the encodings now spell that way.
Rev 10 writes ASM-23, which extends the band-and-shape rule ASM-15 states for seconds to **every** figure a build
moves — a rating value, a cell's counts, a wall clock, a per-hand rate — and names the one place an exact figure
belongs (`protocol.md` §2). Rev 11 writes ASM-24, which settles what a split's redirect is for: the
engine's three splits left 25 citations naming a redirect instead of the rule's carrier, and the rule
that a citation names the carrier is now applied to them — the document sites by the layer that owns
each document, the sites in a `src/` or `tests/` comment, docstring or assertion message left to a code pass — with the redirects kept as
the safety net they were meant to be rather than as a way of addressing a rule (ASM-22,
`docs/L1/README.md`).

| ASM-6 | The fixed game is parameterised as two seats, blinds 1/2 with one blind level that never rises, starting stacks 20 chips, fixed bets of 2 and 4, and a raise cap of 4 per street — inside ASM-1's short-stack direction, and small enough that a hand is a handful of decisions (D-2). The small bet of 2 is the preflop and flop bet and the big bet of 4 the turn and river bet. **The cap is four bet-or-raise actions on a street**, and it is not "the opening bet plus three raises" on every street: post-flop the four are the opening bet and three raises, while pre-flop the posted blind is a posting and not one of the four, so the blind is followed by **four raises — five bet levels in all** (`preflop seat 0 raises to 4`, `… seat 1 raises to 6`, `… seat 0 raises to 8`, `… seat 1 raises to 10` is a capped street, and at the cap `raise` leaves the legal set). `engine.readout`'s second `limit` entry counts those bet-or-raise actions, so it ranges **0–4 on every street** — not the 0–3 a raise-only reading gives — and the dense encoding's divisor for that slot is its own maximum, **4** (D-2, `e2e-surface.md` §2) | Deep stacks of about 100 chips with an uncapped number of raises: it multiplies the per-hand decision count for no gain in what Phase 1 compares, and it multiplies the pure-Python engine cost that C-C2 flags and ASM-15 prices by measurement in ASM-21's band |
| ASM-7 | **The answer to ASM-3.** One learning rule for the whole ladder: masked Q-learning over the three-index action vocabulary, with the reward, exploration schedule, optimiser family, batch size and target-update interval held fixed. The function approximator is hand-rolled in NumPy at R1 and in PyTorch from R2 on (D-7), so R1 and R2 are the same model on the same work and R3–R6 change the representation or the architecture only (D-3) | ASM-3's suggested tabular R1: a table cannot carry "a learned card vector", so R3 would have to introduce both an embedding table and a function approximator in one rung — two changes at once, which D-3 forbids and V-F7 warns about. Also rejected: a policy-gradient method from R2 on, which changes the learning rule on the rung where the network first appears |
| ASM-8 | **Revised at Rev 2.** Budgets are matched in **samples**, not wall-clock: 1,000 hands per variant per seed live and 200,000 for the shipped runs, and the comparison is measured at one table budget — 120 hands per cell live, 1,500 full — at the three fixed seeds of `protocol.md` §2 (D-6, D-8, D-16) | A wall-clock-matched protocol: each rung would then train on a different number of hands, so the comparison would no longer be sample-matched and a slower rung would be reported as worse for being slow (D-6, C-C13) |
| ASM-9 | R4, R5 and R6 each declare a **pair** of variants at one budget, and the pair is the rung's own control — wide against deep, dense against sparse, residual against plain (D-3, D-6) | One configuration per rung: then "width vs depth" is never compared, and a rung would be reported on a curve alone, which D-6 forbids and C-C8 names as naming a technique instead of demonstrating one |
| ASM-10 | A comparison pair is matched to 10,000 trainable parameters within ±2%, measured by `agents.param_count`, so width, depth, density and the residual path are varied at one capacity (D-3, C-C13) | Leaving each variant to pick its own size: width, depth, density and residual would then be confounded with capacity, and the axis the rung is named for could not be read from the result |
| ASM-11 | Path ownership is a declaration, not authorship: `pyproject.toml`, the lock file, `tests/conftest.py` and `tests/e2e/` are claimed by `harness`, and the shipped tables by `evaluation` under `tables/`, so no changed path escapes ownership, while the e2e battery is authored at L1 and the diff-scope check runs over paths inside module subtrees (modules.md §4, D-13) | Leaving root packaging, the battery or the shipped table unclaimed: a scaffolding or table change would then fail the diff-scope check as "owned by no module", and the only alternatives are a lint exemption or a module that exists to own files |
| ASM-12 | The reward is the seat's net chip delta for the completed hand, delivered at the terminal decision, with no shaping and no intermediate term — the zero-sum payoff of `engine.payoffs` (D-6) | Shaped per-decision rewards: a shaped reward is a second thing that would vary between rungs, and it biases the comparison the ladder exists to make (D-3, D-6) |
| ASM-14 | **The answer to ASM-13 on size.** The pool keeps M = 5 versions plus the three pinned baselines; a table's membership is that pool plus the rung's variants, so K is 4 for R1, 9 or 10 once the window is full, and the table has K(K−1)/2 cells — 45 at worst. A cell is 120 hands live and 1,500 full, seats alternating, at one hand count for every pair (D-16) | A larger M, or larger cells, at the same cost: M = 20 or 2,000-hand cells would put the live table past the minutes D-8 allows, and a table of a few dozen hands per cell would report rank differences that are noise. Also rejected: a separate, smaller membership for the live table, which would make the live and full tables two designs and the live one unreadable against the full one (D-16, D-8) |
| ASM-15 | **The answer to ASM-13 on cost, amended to the brief's ASM-21.** The per-hand cost is a **measurement, not a bound**, so this assumption fixes its **band and its shape and no figure of its own**: **0.105–0.153 s** of CPU per hand across the three measured artefacts (6.5–9.5 hands/s) — ASM-21's 0.105–0.120 for a live run, 0.1299–0.1324 for the shipped full table and 0.1415–0.1532 for the live table — because the oracle's equity sample is nearly all of a table's cost and a K = 4 table has it in half its hands while the run faces it in one hand in three (an oracle-bearing cell measured 0.255–0.315 s/hand against 0.002–0.004 for an oracle-free one). Each artefact records its own seconds in its own shape — a run's `timings`, the full table's `cost.seconds_per_hand`, the live table's `stages.matrix_live` over 720 hands — and **the values are read from the artefact** (`checkpoints/evidence/r1-live.json`, `tables/league-full.json`) **and never from a document** — the one exception being the generation-labelled page ASM-23 names, which quotes its own generation and says which one — because a regeneration moves every second-count and leaves only the band (`protocol.md` §2, `strata/walkthrough.md` §7). At the measured band the live table is ≈95–110 s at K = 4 and ≈10–12 minutes at K = 9–10 (4,320–5,400 hands), and the full table ≈2 hours (2.5 h at K = 10 at the shipped rate); holding the band's slow end — the live table's 0.1532 s/hand in ASM-21's own sub-band — instead is the conservative bound — ≈14 minutes at K = 10 live and ≈2.3–2.9 hours full, an over-statement because a wider table pairs the oracle in a smaller share of its hands — the difference between a talk and an offline job that D-8 asks for. `harness.timings` reports the real figure and FAM-10 asserts the record, never a wall clock (ASM-21, ASM-14, D-16, D-8) | Keeping the earlier stated bound of 40 ms of CPU per hand operative (at most 24 decisions at about 1.5 ms per decision): the arithmetic did not price the oracle's equity query at every decision, so it was 3.2× optimistic and would report a ten-minute table as under four minutes (ASM-21). Also rejected: promoting one build's measured seconds to this assumption's figure, which the next regeneration invalidates — the one-build's-seconds drift this assumption closes, which was found in four documents (`strata/walkthrough.md` §5, `protocol.md` §2). And rejected: deriving the cell size from a wall-clock target, which would make the table's resolution depend on the machine the talk runs on, and buying cost back with concurrency, which one global `random` stream forbids (D-16, ASM-2) |
| ASM-16 | **The frozen-pool reading of D-16.** A rung's pool is the last M versions of the rung it inherits plus the baselines, frozen before either arm trains; both arms of a pair therefore sample one pool and record one pool id, and a rung's own snapshots become the next rung's window (D-16, D-3) | A within-run rolling pool in which each arm's own snapshots enter its own training pool: the two arms would then train against different opponents and different data distributions, so D-16's "identical frozen pool" would be false and a pair's difference would no longer be one axis (D-3, D-16) |
| ASM-17 | **The rating.** Bradley-Terry fitted on the cells' win/tie/loss counts with ties halved, a 95% interval from a seeded hand-level bootstrap, anchored so the three baselines' mean rating is 0, with a bounded rating for a member that won or lost everything; the raw matrix is reported alongside because the fit assumes a transitivity poker payoffs need not have (D-16) | Elo: an online update is order-dependent and needs a k-factor chosen at L1, so the rating would carry a schedule artefact the matrix does not have (D-16). Also rejected: a rating over mean chip delta rather than hand outcomes, which is not a win probability and would let one large pot outvote many hands (ASM-12, D-16) |
| ASM-18 | **The snapshots.** A run writes ten equally spaced intra-run snapshots plus its final model, and the pool's window retains the last M of them; `training.checkpoints` indexes exactly the pool plus each variant's final model — so a run **writes 33** paths for three seeds and the repository **ships 18** (six per pair: the retained last five plus that pair's final), the written ones regenerable by any later run and the shipped ones the set a reader protects (D-16, ASM-5, `protocol.md` §5) | Emitting exactly M snapshots: the window would never roll, so "bounded rolling" would be a word for a fixed set (D-16). Also rejected: a snapshot per update, which makes checkpoint churn dominate a live run's wall clock (D-8, C-C2). Also rejected: shipping all ten snapshots of every pair, which makes the repository carry a run's whole churn instead of the league's pool and the final models — the 33-path set a run writes is not the 18-artefact set the index declares (`protocol.md` §5) |
| ASM-19 | **The league's owner.** The pool, the scheduler, the matrix and the rating are `evaluation`'s, so one object is both the training opposition and the evaluation opposition; the map stays at six modules (D-16, D-13) | A seventh `league` module: it would own no data `evaluation` does not, split the pool's membership from its meaning across two owners, add a hop in front of `evaluation`'s own match loop, and give one more agent a claim on the same checkpoints (D-16, D-13) |
| ASM-20 | **A checkpoint records the seed and the encoding it was built at, so a member is loadable from its path alone.** The container carries both facts and the reading of them is the policy builder's: `evaluation.policy_from(path)` takes a path and no seed, reads exactly those two facts from the file — what `agents`' restore is required to recover from the file alone and its surface declines to hand back — and hands the recorded seed to `agents.as_policy` over `agents.load(None, path)`, while the learner, its parameters, its rule and its format version stay `agents.load`'s alone; a file that records no seed or no encoding name, or records one in a form this module does not read, is refused by name and **no seed is ever substituted, a path digest included** (D-16, ASM-2, ASM-5, ASM-18, `records.md` §7, `protocol.md` §5). Written at Rev 6 to resolve a citation three documents and the module itself already made (`docs/L2/evaluation/surface.md` §12, `docs/L2/evaluation/league.md` §7, `docs/L3/evaluation/scheduler.md` §5.1) | A seed beside the path in the builder's signature — a `policy_from(path, seed=…)` — or a seed derived from the path: either is a second, unchecked claim about which learner the file holds, and a table could then replay a member at a seed the member's own record contradicts. Also rejected: having `agents` hand the two facts back through its surface so the caller need not read the file, which would put one module's reader inside another's restore and expose a learner's internals on the granted arrow (D-13, `agents/surface.md` §10) |
| ASM-22 | **A document cites the rule's carrier — a `D-n`, an `ASM-n` or a document section — and never `strata/records/phase1-walkthrough.md`.** That record is replaced on every run — git holds the replaced copies (`strata/walkthrough.md` §7) — so a citation into it dangles the moment the next run lands, and naming the finding's **subject** does not save it: the sentence survives the replacement while the record it points into is gone, or now carries an unrelated finding of a later run (D-14, `strata/walkthrough.md` §7, `docs/L1/README.md`). A rule that has no carrier yet gets one at L1 before any layer cites it — an `ASM-n` here, or the section of the L1 document that owns the question, as `battery.md` §2 owns where a battery writes and `ladder.md` §7 owns where a notebook writes | Rev 8's subject form — "the write-root finding in `strata/records/phase1-walkthrough.md`" — which is what five sites below this layer had written and what stops resolving as soon as the next run replaces the record. Also rejected: an archival record per run beside the live one, which is a second record to keep true, and no procedure asks for it (D-14) |
| ASM-23 | **Prose states the shape of a result and the band it lives in, and points at the artefact for the number.** A rating is described by its **ordering and its separation** — "ranks top, its interval clear of the baselines" — a cell by its **direction** — "loses to it, and cites the file" — and a cost by its **band plus the artefact**, because every one of those figures is a measurement that a regeneration moves while the ordering, the directions and the band do not: the two shipped artefacts have already carried three generations of rating values, cell counts and wall clocks under one unchanged shape, and each generation made the prose that quoted its predecessor wrong. One exception and only one: a document whose **whole purpose is to describe one generation of one artefact, identified by that generation's commit and its `generated_utc`**, quotes that generation exactly and says which one it is — `docs/L3/training/evidence.md` §3 is the example, and a page that loses that label loses the licence. Where a page needs a number for rhetorical force it gives an approximation a re-run cannot falsify — a rounded value or an inequality — never a figure a re-run will move. What the rule does **not** forbid is a **forecast**: D-17's expected wall clock for a measurement the reader runs on their own machine is a band or an approximation with the measurement it derives from named beside it, and never a build's observed seconds dressed as a prediction of someone else's (D-17, ASM-15, `protocol.md` §2, `league.md` §7, `checkpoints/evidence/r1-live.json`, `tables/league-full.json`) | Quoting the shipped artefacts' exact values in prose, as `league.md` §8's rating figures, `docs/L2/evaluation/league.md` §5's wall clock and `docs/L3/evaluation/tables.md` §1's did: each was correct when written and stale after the next regeneration, and a reader could not tell a current number from a remembered one — which is the same defect three times. Also rejected: a generation note on every figure instead of one labelled document, which spreads the exception across pages that do not date themselves and so never say which build they mean (D-6, D-8) |
| ASM-24 | **A split's redirect is a pointer, not the rule's carrier.** When a document passes its ceiling and a body moves to the document that owns the question, the vacated section stays behind so no existing citation dangles — and that is all it is: a safety net for a reference from outside this tree, never an address inside it. This is a test over **any** redirect the tree creates, not a list of the four it happens to have: the three older ones' patterns return 30 lines, less 3 `Split from:` lines and 2 self-naming sentences, so 25 citations — 20 in the shadow of `docs/L3/engine/advance.md` §2, 3 of `docs/L3/engine/equity.md` §3, 2 of `docs/L3/engine/guardrails.md` G6 — of which 8 were swept in a document (6 in `docs/L3/engine/`, 2 in `docs/L3/features/README.md`); this round's own split added 10 at `docs/L3/engine/errors.md` §3, 1 of them swept in `docs/L3/engine/actions.md`. ## What was not decided here

Nothing in this map needs the user: the algorithm was delegated to L1 by ASM-3, the league's sizes were
delegated by ASM-13, and the choice between Elo and Bradley-Terry was offered as a choice by D-16. The
rung order, the game, the three baselines and the existence of the league are taken as given (D-2, D-3,
D-6, D-16) and are not re-litigated.
