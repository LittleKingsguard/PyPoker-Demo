# PyPoker-Demo — L1 assumptions

- **Layer:** L1 · **Unit:** phase1 · **Rev:** 15 · **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Purpose:** the decisions L1 made where the brief left the answer to this layer, each with the alternative it rejected
- **Ceiling:** 110 lines. At the ceiling, split by owner — never append

ASM-1 to ASM-5 and ASM-13 are the brief's own open assumptions and are not restated here: ASM-3
delegates the learning algorithm to L1 and its answer is ASM-7; ASM-13 asks L1 for the pool size, the
hands per pairing and the match count, and its answers are ASM-14 and ASM-15. ASM-6..ASM-19 are Rev 1's
and Rev 2's; each is a candidate for ratification into the brief's §7 by L0. An id is kept across
revisions so every citation to it still resolves: Rev 3–Rev 7 reshape ASM-15 to the band and the shape
the brief's ASM-21 ratified from measurement and send the reader to the artefact for the seconds
(`protocol.md` §2), Rev 6 writes ASM-20 (a checkpoint records the seed and the encoding it was built at),
Rev 8–Rev 9 write ASM-22 (a citation names the rule's carrier, never a walkthrough record) and correct
ASM-6's cap gloss, Rev 10 writes ASM-23 (a document states a result's shape and its band and points at
the artefact for the number), Rev 11 writes ASM-24 (a split's redirect is a pointer, not the rule's
carrier), Rev 12 writes ASM-25–ASM-27 for D-19's rule catalogue, and Rev 13 restates ASM-12 and ASM-27
and writes ASM-28–ASM-29 for D-20's utility catalogue. Rev 14 answers D-21: ASM-30 carries the hand-class
fact through the readout rather than through a new entry point, ASM-31 pins what a folding baseline does
where D-21's rule meets the game's legality, ASM-32 keeps the rating's anchor on the three baselines of
D-6, and ASM-33 states what the full declared set does to K, to the cells and to the tables' cost,
and ASM-34 pins the one of the three rules this game cannot fire and states why rather than repairing it
(`folders.md` §2–§6, D-21). Rev 15 answers D-29: ASM-35 declares the second game shape and the one endpoint it
costs, ASM-36 pins the sized vocabulary in legal amounts, ASM-37 the index order that cannot be read as a
preference, ASM-38 what a run retains and the greedy surface it is read through, ASM-39 the encoding's
treatment of size, ASM-40 the head and the container version, and ASM-41 the criterion pinned before any run
(`shapes.md` §1–§7, `sizing.md` §1–§7).

| ASM-25 | **The second rule, and the third that was declined (D-19).** The catalogue holds exactly two members: `masked-q-learning`, which bootstraps from the masked maximum of the target copy's values, and `double-q-learning`, which lets the online parameters choose the index of the bootstrap and reads that index's value from the target copy — the decoupling of action selection from action evaluation that the diagnosis names as the remedy for an upward-biased maximum (D-19, `strata/records/r2-plateau-diagnosis.md`, `rules.md` §2). A **returns-based** third rule — Monte-Carlo or n-step, D-18's other named candidate — is declined: a return is defined over an episode and the transition record carries one decision and its successor with no episode boundary (`records.md` §4), so accepting any batch would make the rule learn nothing from a one-transition batch and refusing a batch without a terminal transition would give the catalogue members different **admissible inputs**, an interface difference D-19 forbids. No fourth is added (`rules.md` §5) | A returns-based third rule that refuses a terminal-less batch: the catalogue's members would then disagree about what `learn` accepts, so `training` would have to branch on the rule — the one thing D-19's shared contract exists to prevent. | ASM-26 | **A pool that holds several estimators: its order, its rolling and its eviction (D-19).** The window's versions are grouped into **families**: a family is one lineage chain, every member of it declares one rule, and its label is the start its first member names. The window lists each family's members contiguously, oldest first, and the families in the order they entered it — for a declared membership, the order of each family's start in the declaration. `offer` extends exactly **one** family: the offered version's `lineage` must be the id of a member that is the newest of its family, or a label no member carries, which opens a new family; a version whose rule differs from the family it extends is refused, and so is a lineage that names a member which is not its family's newest, because the chain would fork. When the window already holds `m` versions, the one that leaves is **the oldest member of the family that currently holds the most members**, counting the version that has just joined, ties broken by which family's oldest member is the older — so no family is emptied while another holds two or more, and heterogeneity survives the bound: with F families resident, each keeps at least one member while F ≤ m, and an offer that would open the (m+1)th family is refused by name rather than emptying one. `build_pool` windows a too-rich declaration by the same rule and refuses a declaration holding more than `m` families (`league.md` §2, `docs/L3/evaluation/pool.md`) | **Oldest-first over the concatenated window**, which is the rule before D-19 and which can silently evict the last member of a family: the population would stop holding an estimator while the table still claimed to have fitted over it, and the run trained against a pool that no longer matched its own record (D-19, ASM-16). Also rejected: **one pool per rule**, which is two matrices and two ratings, and a rating cannot be fitted over two disjoint memberships with one anchor (D-16). Also rejected: **raising M** to hold every family's chain in full, which is D-16's and ASM-14's cost decision — cells rise as K(K−1)/2 — and not this assumption's |

| ASM-28 | **The utility catalogue (D-20).** The terminal reward becomes a per-variant **utility** selected exactly as the learning rule is (ASM-7): `agents.learners()["utilities"]` publishes **`net-chips`** — ASM-12's unshaped net chip delta, unchanged, and the declared default — and **`stack-shortfall`**, whose terminal reward is `(U(s_before) − U(s_after)) / U(s_0)` with `U(s) = (T − s)²`, `T` the total chips in play, `s_0` the stack a hand begins from and `s_before`/`s_after` the seat's stack before and after the outcome, delivered at the terminal decision only, with no intermediate term and no shaping. The transform is applied where the reward is **computed** — `training`'s transition record — and not in `learn`'s terminal branch: the utility needs a completed hand's stacks and payoff while a transition record carries one decision and its successor, and a utility that changed what `learn` admits would give the catalogue's members different interfaces, which is the defect ASM-25 refuses for the rules (`utilities.md` §3, §4). `T` and `s_0` are read, not assumed — the readout's committed-and-held chips at the terminal decision against the game's declared starting stack — and a hand whose two totals disagree is refused by name. The two utilities are **one capacity**: one state layout, one `param_count`, one checkpoint array (ASM-10). The asymmetry is arithmetic and no claim about play: at a 20-chip stack a ±2 pair scores +0.190 / −0.210 and an all-in +1.000 / −3.000, so a loss of a given size costs more than a win of the same size pays (D-20, ASM-6) | A **scalar rescaling or clipping of the net chip delta** — a reward divided by 10, or clamped at ±2 — which changes no preference among equal-EV actions, and changing the preference among equal-EV actions is the whole of D-20's purpose (`strata/records/r2-plateau-diagnosis.md`). ## What was not decided here

Nothing in this map needs the user: the algorithm was delegated to L1 by ASM-3, the league's sizes were
delegated by ASM-13, and the choice between Elo and Bradley-Terry was offered as a choice by D-16. The
rung order, the three baselines and the existence of the league are taken as given (D-2, D-3,
D-6, D-16) and are not re-litigated; the game is now two declared shapes, and which one a configuration
names is data (D-29, ASM-35).

**What D-19 leaves to the user is its re-baseline, and L1 has not taken it.** Which rules the *ladder*
declares — R1–R6 each declaring the two-rule set rather than the one rule they declare today — is L0's
call, because it doubles every rung's trained variants, makes every rung's table K = 10, and re-measures
every shipped artefact; this layer builds the mechanism and proves it at the live budget, and states the
cost of that decision in its return rather than assuming it (D-19, D-8, ASM-8).

**What D-20 leaves to the user is the same call for the utility, and L1 has not taken it either.** The six
rungs declare `net-chips` for both arms, so no rung asks its two arms different questions and no shipped
artefact is re-measured; making a rung declare `stack-shortfall`, or adding a seventh rung whose axis is the
utility, is L0's, for the reason D-19's is: it changes what a rung's table measures and re-baselines the
evidence under it (D-20, D-8, `utilities.md` §10). What L1 has done is build the axis and prove it at the live
budget with two variants that differ only in utility (D-20, ASM-23).

**What D-21 leaves to the user is its re-baseline, and L1 has not taken that either.** The six registry rows
declare the three anchor baselines and no folder, so no shipped artefact is re-measured by this change and
`tables/league-full.json` and `checkpoints/evidence/r1-live.json` keep their membership, their cells and
their ratings (D-21, `protocol.md` §5). Declaring the folders in a rung — which takes that rung's table to
K = 12 or 13 and re-measures it — is L0's, for the reason D-19's and D-20's re-baselines are: it changes
what a rung's table measures. What L1 has done is build the catalogue, the fact and the membership, and
prove them at the live budget with two runs that differ only in the pool they face (`folders.md` §6,
ASM-33).

**What D-29 leaves to the user, and what it leaves to a later build.** Four things are not this layer's and are
stated rather than taken: the **four supersessions** the second shape requires — D-2, D-3, D-4 and ASM-6, named
in `shapes.md` §7 — are the register's to file, and the register is not L1's write scope; `modules.json`'s
`engine` row must be re-declared 9 → 10 in the same change, which is a one-line edit this layer does not own
(`shapes.md` §4); whether a **rung** declares the second shape is L0's, for the reason D-19's, D-20's and
D-21's re-baselines are — it would re-measure a rung's table on a second game — and this layer has designed the
configuration route instead; and the **overnight run** stays gated on ASM-41's criterion being met at the live
budget, so no build is authorised by this design (D-29, D-26, D-28).
