# PyPoker-Demo — L1 the revealed counterfactual

- **Layer:** L1 · **Unit:** phase1 · **Rev:** 1 · **Date:** 2026-11-11
- **Covers:** D-43 — the **resolution** the records gain (the episode boundary D-41 said was missing and the reveal D-40 measured), the **method** chosen for the counterfactual and what it costs, its arithmetic and the proof that it is **one quantity**, every action it values with the continuation assumption each declares, the **axis** it rides and the budget consequence of riding it, the **criterion** pinned before any run, and the **shapes** it is declared on
- **Ceiling:** 130 lines. At the ceiling, split by owner — never append

## 1. What D-43 obliges, and where each answer is

D-43 overrides D-41's refusal and authorises the surface change D-41 named: the transition record gains an **episode boundary**, the episode record gains the **reveal**, and the counterfactual is then resolved from declared artefacts and never from a privileged peek at decision time. Its seven requirements are answered here in order: resolution and what it costs (§2), the method and the cost of choosing it (§3), one quantity and the proof (§4), all the actions and the assumption each declares (§5), the axis and the budget (§6), the criterion pinned before any run (§7), and the shapes (§8). What does not move is §9. D-40 stays a **measurement** (its five eliminations and its 87 %-both-arms result stand) and D-41's four structural findings are the build's requirement list, not obstacles to route around.

## 2. The resolution: what the records gain, its bound, and what it costs

Five keys are added across the two records and no fact is added to the readout. Four are **required** — `episode` on the transition record, and `reveal`, `amounts` and `counterfactual` where each belongs — and the fifth, the transition's `counterfactual`, is **conditional**, because a transition only carries values when its episode resolved. D-43's demand is that a counterfactual be resolvable from the records, and the readout may not be widened to make it so (`docs/L3/engine/readout.md` §4, D-3).

- **`records.md` §4's transition record gains `episode`** — **required**, an `int`: the episode's own `seed` (`records.md` §5), so two transitions of one hand carry one number and two hands of one run carry two. One episode's transitions are contiguous in the batch and the batch's episodes are in the order the hands were played. Bound: one integer per transition. This is the field D-41 recorded as missing and `rules.md` §5 as declined; it is also what makes an **episode-scoped target expressible for every catalogue member**, which is why the boundary is required of `learn` rather than of one member (`rules.md` §5).
- **`records.md` §5's episode record gains `reveal`** — **required**, written when the hand ends and read only by the step that builds transitions: `board` (the board as dealt, 0–5 card spellings), `showdown` (whether two or more seats showed), `split` (whether the shown hands tie for best), and `seats`, one entry per seat **in seat order**, each `seat`, `hole` (that seat's two cards, however the seat finished), `shown` (whether its cards were revealed, which is to say it was live at the end and never folded) and `won` (whether its `hole` plus `board` is at least as good as **every shown seat's**). Bound: `seats` entries of four facts and two card spellings each, plus five board cards — at most `2·seats + 5` card spellings per hand, 9 on the default shape and 17 on the six-seat one, and `seats + 3` other facts. `won` is defined against the shown seats and not against the winners, because the seat it is asked about may have folded: that is the one question a counterfactual at a fold needs. **Each seat's net delta is the episode record's own `payoffs`**, the required key it already carries and sums to zero (`protocol.md` §2), and the reveal does not repeat it: D-43's clause is answered by the pair — the reveal says which seats were revealed, with what, on which board and whose cards were best, and `payoffs` says what each seat netted — so the outcome is stated once and can be checked against itself rather than twice and drifting.
- **`records.md` §5's decision record gains `amounts` and `counterfactual`**, both from the engine and both bounded by the shape's descriptor count `A` (3 on `fixed-limit-20`, 6 on the two uncapped rows). `amounts` maps every **legal** descriptor to the raise-to it would have resolved to and `None` for `fold` and `check-call` — the engine's own band arithmetic, never a second spelling of it in `training` (`sizing.md` §2, D-13). `counterfactual` maps every legal descriptor to that action's whole-hand net chip delta for the acting seat under §4's closure, or `None` in a hand §4 refuses to resolve. Both are per decision and per action, so neither is a hand-level fact the readout could carry.

**What it costs.** The episode record grows by at most 17 card spellings and 6 sub-mappings per hand — roughly 0.3 KB at six seats and 0.15 KB at two — and each decision record by at most `A` amounts and `A` integers, ~50 bytes at six seats. **No run record grows**: a run retains no episode (D-28, `records.md` §15), so the curves, the pool, the greedy block and every checkpoint keep their sizes, and §9's byte-identity is a measurement rather than a hope. The engine pays one best-five search per seat on the final board (the search `readout.md` §2 already prices at 2.1 µs on the flop, 34.5 µs on the turn and 101 µs on the river) and `A` integer comparisons per decision; the reveal is computed once per hand however many decisions it holds.

**What the readout does not gain.** Nothing: the eleven facts are closed, no twelfth is added, no encoding carries a card another seat holds, and a learner's observation is the same observation it had before (`e2e-surface.md` §2, `readout.md` §4, FAM-2). The reveal is post-hand data consumed by the training step; at decision time no policy can reach it, because `engine.readout` returns before the hand ends and the reveal is written after it.

## 3. The method: the reveal, and why not the replay

D-43 sanctions either form and leaves the choice here. **The reveal is chosen; the replay is rejected on cost.** Both were available — `engine.new_hand`/`apply_action` let a caller re-drive a hand from any decision with one action substituted, and D-43's own last clause says so — and the difference is what each must pay per hand.

- **Replay** must, for every decision the learner took and every legal action at it, re-deal the hand, re-apply the prefix, substitute the action, and then continue to a real payoff against the hand's opponents. It is exact for folds, showdowns, bluffs and calls alike, and it needs no assumption. Its cost is `D × A` re-drives of the rest of the hand: with the learner's own decisions `D` and the shape's `A` actions, that is 12 re-drives a hand at `A = 3` and 4 decisions and 24 at `A = 6`, each of them a full continuation through `engine.readout` and the seated policies — and an oracle-bearing hand is the expensive kind (ASM-21, ASM-15).
- **The reveal** pays one hand-level resolution: one best-five search per seat on the final board, and `A − 1` integer comparisons per decision. It is **exact** for `fold` and for a live action on a hand that reached a showdown after the river, and it resolves **nothing** for a hand that ended without one — where the member falls back to the rule's own target, exactly as D-40 pins.

**The declared cost of the choice, measured once for this design on this host** (one build, one machine; a re-run moves the numbers and not the ratio): a hand at six seats costs ≈**0.18 s**, of which a single oracle decision is ≈**0.177 s** and a policy decision ≈5 µs; the board-and-hands resolution the reveal needs costs ≈**55 µs per seat** — ≈**0.33 ms a hand** at six seats — and `A` integers a decision; a **replay** of the remainder costs ≈**3.7 ms** (3.0–5.6 ms) when its continuation meets no oracle decision, and the count is **16–17 replays a hand** at `A = 6`, so ≈**0.07 s a hand** in an oracle-free continuation and ≈**3 s a hand** as soon as one replay's continuation draws an oracle decision — against a 0.18 s hand. That is the whole argument: the reveal costs **0.2 %** of a hand, the replay **36 %** of one without the oracle in it and **sixteen hands' worth** with it, and the pool's pinned oracle sits in most six-seat hands (`records.md` §8, D-6). The `A`-step update the member takes costs ≈**64 µs** a transition against the one-step path's ≈19 µs — a **2.4×** transition cost and a **1.25×** cost for the whole `learn` call, because the forward pass is shared. Per hand, therefore, the member adds one resolution and `A` comparisons a decision and no policy call, and the method it *replaces* would have added `D·A` continuations. What the choice gives up is stated in §4 and §5 and is not papered over: a hand without a showdown carries no signal, a split pot carries none, and a counterfactual raise is valued under an assumption because no reveal can observe a response to an action that was never taken.

## 4. One quantity, and the proof that it is one

For a hand `h`, one of the acting seat `s`'s decisions `d`, and one legal action `a` at `d`, let `C = s_0 − stacks[s]` at `d` (the seat's own whole contribution before `d`, read off the readout's `stacks` and `pot`, so it is the same chips ASM-12 counts), `c(a)` the chips `a` commits at `d` (0 for `fold`, `to_call` for `check-call`, `amounts[a]` less the seat's own bet on the street for a raise), `Δ` the seat's realised net chip delta for the completed hand (`payoffs[s]` — the number the engine places at the seat's terminal decision and the one the utility consumes), and `O` the chips **every other seat** contributed to the whole hand.

**The closure.** A deviation moves the seat's own chips, moves **no other seat's** chips, and moves **no card**: the deck is fixed at the deal, so the board and every opponent's hand are the realised ones, and `O` is invariant. Under it, with `won` the reveal's fact for that seat and `shown` the reveal's fact for whether the seat was still live at the end:

| Action at `d` | Its value |
|---|---|
| `fold` | `−C` |
| `check-call`, or any raise band, when `won` **and** `shown` | `O` |
| `check-call`, or any raise band, otherwise | `−(C + c(a) + later)` |

The `shown` conjunct is not decoration and it is not a second spelling of anything: a seat that makes a live decision and then **folds on a later street** is not live at the end, so the branch at that earlier decision still ends in a fold, commits `C + c(a) + later` and wins nothing — while its cards may well have been the best, which is the one thing `won` alone reports. Without `shown` that decision would be valued at `O` and the identity below would be false exactly there.

`later` is the seat's own realised commitments after `d`, which the deviation leaves alone (`0` whenever `d` is the seat's last decision, which is the common case). Both branches are **whole-hand**: `O` and `later` are the completed hand's, the seat's contribution is counted once, and no branch charges `to_call` where another does not. That is the arithmetic defect the gate recorded (F5/C8) and this is its repair.

**The proof.** If `a` is the action the hand actually applied at `d`, then `c(a)` **is** the realised commitment at `d`, so the branch contributes exactly what the seat contributed and wins exactly what it won: `won` with `shown` gives `O = Δ`, and its negation gives `−(C + c(a) + later) = Δ`. Hence **`v(a_realised) = Δ` at every transition of a resolved episode, under either utility** — the utility is applied to both sides by the same function (`utilities.md` §4), so the equality survives the transform. The battery asserts it per transition, over every decision and not only the seat's last, and it is the one invariant that catches a branch charging a cost the other omits (FAM-22).

Two bounds are **assumptions and never findings**, declared here because no reveal can supply them: the closure credits a raise with no fold-out, so a **bluff is lower-bounded** by the showdown it would have lost; and it omits the chips a caller would match, so a **winning raise's** value is understated by exactly those chips. Two kinds of hand carry **no ledger at all**, and both are checked rather than assumed: a hand whose shown hands **tie for best** is `split`; and a hand in which **any seat's realised winnings are neither zero nor the whole of `O`** is one where the pot was built in levels — an all-in for less takes a side pot, and a seat is then paid by its eligibility as well as by its rank — so reconstructing the share here would be a second spelling of the engine's own pot-building and of its odd-chip rule (`docs/L3/engine/hand.md` §5). The second condition is the resolution's own **self-check**: it is exactly the statement that the closure's `O`-or-zero arithmetic reproduces what the hand paid, so a hand that would break the identity carries no values instead of wrong ones.

## 5. All the actions, and what each one assumes

`counterfactual` covers **every legal index at that decision and no others**: `fold`, `check-call`, and **every raise band the shape declares** — the one `raise` of `fixed-limit-20` (its minimum completion, unique in that shape) and all four bands of the two uncapped rows, each resolved by `amounts` (`sizing.md` §2, ASM-36). An index that was not legal carries `None`, so a masked action is never valued and never updated (`agents/rule.md` §2). `fold` is exact and needs no assumption: a fold forfeits the pot and commits nothing further. `check-call` is exact on a resolved hand: its commitment is `to_call`, its outcome is the reveal's, and `shown` decides whether the seat stays in it. A raise band is exact in its **own** commitment and assumption-bound in the opponent's response, as §4 states. No action is left out because a band was untaken: the untaken arms are the whole point, since a learner that never folds has no fold sample to learn from (`strata/records/counterfactual-probe.md`, D-39).

## 6. The axis it rides, and what it costs the budget

**It is neither a rule nor a utility; it is a third catalogue.** `agents.learners()` publishes `counterfactuals`, in the declared order, with `none` first and the declared default — the rule's own target, unchanged — and `reveal-counterfactual` second, which takes its residual from the transition's `counterfactual` instead of from a bootstrap. Both alternatives were considered and are wrong: a **utility** owns one terminal number and is read by `training` and never by `learn` (`utilities.md` §3, §4), while this is `A` numbers per decision that `learn` must read; and a **rule** owns the **bootstrap** and nothing else (`rules.md` §3), while this member reads no successor at all — it would leave `rules.md` §2's table describing a member with no bootstrap and would put a non-TD estimator on D-19's axis, whose whole purpose was comparing two bootstrap remedies for overestimation. This is the shape the gate found neither catalogue fits (D-41 F7), and the answer is a third catalogue rather than a forced fit. It rides **opt-in per variant** — a variant entry's `counterfactual`, absent meaning `none` — so a paired arm differs only in it, on one frozen pool, one seed set and one budget (D-3). A variant declaring it, or a version member trained under it, carries the name in the container (**format version 5**, required field; versions 1–4 read as `none`), in the run record's per-variant row and `counterfactuals` set, and in the pool member's record; the pool **id** digests it only when it is not `none`, so no shipped id moves (ASM-27's rule for the baselines, applied a third time).

**Its residual is a mean, and its contract says so.** One transition's parameter movement is the **mean** over the steps it takes and not their sum: each step's residual is `(v_i − q[i]) / A`. That need is **measured**, not stylistic. The member's residuals are whole-hand chip deltas — ±hundreds on a 600-chip shape — where the rule it replaces bootstraps from near-zero values and so starts with residuals near zero, and `A` steps of a full residual move the parameters `A` times as far as the rule's own single step. Run that way on `six-seat-no-limit-100` at the declared `learning_rate`, the learner drove a parameter out of finite range inside a hundred hands (`LearningDiverged`, naming the transition, the action index and the parameter) — so the member as first built was unusable on the shape this change is measured on. The optimiser stays **plain SGD**: no momentum, no weight decay, no clipping (`docs/L3/agents/rule.md` §3). What the member's contract adds is the **aggregation of its own residuals**, beside its **step count**, and nothing else. Rejected: running at `A` full residuals per transition, which is the divergence above; and clipping a residual to the rule's scale, which is the forbidden "clipping" and would hide the arithmetic behind a bound.

**The budget, and the correction.** `reveal-counterfactual` takes **`A` parameter updates per transition** — one semi-gradient step per legal action, each on the cache of one forward pass so that no residual reads a value another step of the same transition wrote — `A` being the shape's descriptor count, 3 or 6. It takes them at a **terminal** transition exactly as at any other: the seat's last decision of a hand carries the whole-hand value of every legal action and the realised one equals that transition's `reward`, so the terminal one is where the outcome is known and it is not exempt (§4). `rules.md` §2's "a rule swap moves no budget" is therefore **corrected**: a swap on the *rule* axis still moves no budget, and a member of **this** axis states its own **step count and residual aggregation in its contract**. What moves is exactly that: the parameter-update count per hand rises by `A`, the target copy's `target_update_interval` is consumed `A` times faster under this member (200 transitions, not 200 steps), and the `td_error` a curve reads is the batch's mean over its `A·transitions` steps. What does not move is the sample budget: the same hands, the same transitions, the same decisions, the same exploration-schedule position, which advances by decisions and never by steps (`docs/L3/agents/rule.md` §3, §4, ASM-7). **One scope boundary is declared rather than left to be discovered:** no cell and no matrix record names the member in this pass, because the shapes it is declared for a table — a six-seat table refuses a cell — and the paired difference the criterion reads is in the two **run records**, which do name it (`seats.md` §5, D-33).

## 7. The criterion, pinned before any run

Fixed **now**, before any number of this member exists, and computed by a script from the artefacts and never read by eye (D-27, ASM-23, `sizing.md` §7's method).

- **Observable.** The **greedy** census by descriptor of the learner's own decisions in the run record's `greedy` block — `fold`, `check-call` and each raise band — with the **tie-resolved decisions excluded** (`records.md` §15's `tie` flag, ASM-37), and the same block's chips per hand. No census over training decisions is admissible, and a block whose `mode` is not `off` is refused by name (D-28, ASM-38).
- **Seeds and sample.** Seeds **0 and 1**, the user's; **20,000 hands per variant per seed** on `six-seat-no-limit-100`, so each block is the run's own first **2,000** hands (`greedy._sample`, D-37) and each seed contributes at most 98,000 decision records.
- **The two arms.** One configuration, two variants differing in `counterfactual` alone, on one frozen pool of folders, a raiser and callers, one seed set and one budget — so the pool id is one id for both arms and is reported (D-3, D-16, §6).
- **PASS** requires, **per seed, both arms' blocks read at once**: (1) the `reveal-counterfactual` arm's fold share is **≥ 5.0 %** of its tie-excluded decisions; (2) it exceeds the `none` arm's fold share over **the same 2,000 deals** — the two arms replay one prefix, so the comparison is paired — by **≥ 5.0 percentage points**; and (3) both records name `six-seat-no-limit-100`, both blocks carry `mode: "off"`, and both record **one** pool id. Anything else is a FAIL, named with its seed and its conjunct, and a run whose chips improve does not save it (D-24, D-28).
- **Why those numbers.** The measured gap this change exists to close is 87 % against 0 % on one table (`strata/records/reveal-counterfactual.md`, `strata/records/double-q-test.md`, D-40, D-42); 5 points is a threshold a null result cannot reach by accident at the ≈2,000 tie-excluded decisions a six-seat pass of 2,000 hands yields — a six-seat hand gives the learner about one decision, so the pass's decision count is near its hand count (a share's standard error is then ≈1.1 points and 5 points is beyond four of them) — and it is an order of magnitude below the measured gap, so a PASS is a real signal and a FAIL is a real failure. **Chips, the update count and every descriptor's share are reported and never thresholded**: a 4,000-hand chip comparison cannot resolve a small edge (`strata/records/attractor-sweep.md`, ASM-41).
- **Where the verdict lives, and who computes it.** `notebooks/counterfactual-criterion.py` — `training`'s path, as `utilities.md` §10's ablation script is — reads the two run records and writes `checkpoints/evidence/counterfactual-pair.json` **under the write root the ambient `PYPOKER_CHECKPOINT_ROOT` names**, which is that path for a run that is not a battery's, carrying both records' `greedy` blocks, the census, the criterion as pinned here and the verdict with its conjunct and seed. It is a **new** file: no shipped artefact is regenerated (`shapes.md` §5). A PASS authorises nothing beyond the record of a measured effect; the overnight run stays gated as D-26 and D-30 leave it.

## 8. The shapes it is declared on

Declared on **all three** shapes — `fixed-limit-20`, `no-limit-100` and `six-seat-no-limit-100` — because §4's arithmetic reads only `pot`, `stacks`, `to_call`, the decision's own `amounts` and the reveal, all of which every row carries, and because the fixed-limit shape has showdowns too and a hand that reaches one is resolved there exactly as anywhere else. **Measured on `six-seat-no-limit-100` only**, that being the configuration the user named and the one cell where a tabular learner folds 87 % and the product folds 0 % (D-40, D-42). `A` is the shape's descriptor count and the bands are the shape's own, so the member's cost per decision is 3 numbers on the default row and 6 on the others; **no figure from one shape is evidence for another** (D-33 §4, `shapes.md` §6).

## 9. What does not move, and what the map costs

The 18 checkpoints, `checkpoints/evidence/r1-live.json`, `tables/league-full.json`, the six rungs and both deep runs stay Phase 1's fixed-limit evidence, and the fixed-limit path is held to the byte-identical proof the previous builds carried (`shapes.md` §5, `battery.md` §5, D-33 §4). **The additive keys a default declaration adds to a record are: none.** Every key of §2 lives in the episode record or the transition record, neither of which a run retains, and every other carrier is conditional on the member not being `none` — so a fixed-limit run at 1,000 hands reproduces its capture **field for field**: `tests/integration/training/test_default_baseline.py` holds a 24-hand run to its 21-field pre-change capture, and `tests/integration/training/test_greedy_sample.py` holds a **1,000-hand** run to its own 21-field capture in a child pinned to `PYTHONHASHSEED=0`, curves, per-hand chips, the parameter band and each container member's digest included. Neither capture is regenerated and neither comparison takes a tolerance. **No entry point is added**: the map stays at **50** endpoints, every `modules.json` row and harness ceiling stands — `engine` 10/150, `features` 7/120, `agents` 10/150, `evaluation` 11/160, `training` 6/110, `harness` 6/110 — and the reveal, `amounts`, the episode boundary, the third catalogue and its container field all reach the surface as keys of existing records and members of existing catalogues (D-13, brief §5). The battery gains one family, FAM-22, and loses no case (`battery.md` §3, D-11).
