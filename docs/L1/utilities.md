# PyPoker-Demo — L1 utilities: the reward catalogue

- **Layer:** L1 · **Unit:** phase1 · **Rev:** 1 · **Date:** 2026-11-09
- **Covers:** D-20's utility axis — the catalogue, the reward each member delivers, where it is consumed and why there, what every record and container carries, the fairness rule, what a table may not be read as, and the verification D-20 asks for
- **Ceiling:** 110 lines. At the ceiling, split by owner — never append

## 1. Why the reward is a parameter

The terminal reward was one module constant for the whole ladder: the seat's net chip delta, placed at that seat's terminal decision and shaped nowhere (ASM-12). The measured reason it stops being one is `strata/records/r2-plateau-diagnosis.md`: raise and call are **EV-neutral** in this game — the same deals split ±2 or ±20 by the same shares, so the policy changes only the stake — and a max operator over three noisy estimates takes whichever action drew positive noise, which is why the ladder's models converged on raise-almost-always at ±20 where ±2 was equally good. D-20 selects a second objective that scores progress in the square of the stack's shortfall from all the chips in play, so that a loss of a given size costs more than a win of the same size pays. **It is a change of preference among equal-EV options and not a change of the game**: it creates no expectation the game does not have, it does not make the coin flip stop being a coin flip, and a model trained under it is not thereby a stronger player (D-20, D-2).

## 2. The catalogue

`agents.learners()["utilities"]` publishes exactly two members, in this order, each carrying its `name`, the `reward` it delivers as a stable token, and the `normaliser` that reward is divided by. The first is the declared default of §5. Both consume one `hyper` declaration and one state layout, so a utility swap is one axis and no budget moves with it (D-20, D-19, ASM-7, ASM-28).

| Utility | The reward it delivers at the terminal decision | What it reads |
|---|---|---|
| `net-chips` | `net-chip-delta` — the seat's net chip delta for the completed hand, unnormalised (ASM-12) | `engine.payoffs`' payoff for that seat |
| `stack-shortfall` | `squared-stack-shortfall-delta` — `(U(s_before) − U(s_after)) / U(s_0)`, with `U(s) = (T − s)²` | the same payoff, and the chips in play (§4) |

`T` is the total chips in play, `s` one seat's stack, `s_0` the stack a hand begins from, and `s_before`/`s_after` that seat's stack before and after the hand's outcome. At the fixed game both seats begin every hand at 20 chips against a total of 40, so a hand that wins or loses 2 chips scores **+0.190 / −0.210** and one that wins or loses all 20 scores **+1.000 / −3.000**: a loss of a given size always costs more than a win of the same size pays, and an all-in is penalised threefold (D-20, ASM-6, ASM-28).

## 3. The one contract every utility answers

Whatever its utility, a learner is one object built by `agents.build_learner` and it answers the same six calls with the same shapes — `act`, `learn`, `save`, `load`, `param_count`, `as_policy` (`e2e-surface.md` §3.3); the two utilities are **one capacity**, because a utility changes the number a terminal transition carries and nothing of the state layout, so `param_count`, the checkpoint's `2·P + M` array and the ±2% budget match of ASM-10 are identical under either (§2, ASM-10, D-20).

The reward is **terminal only**: every non-terminal decision carries `0.0`, exactly as ASM-12 places it, `learn`'s terminal branch stays `target = reward` and its non-terminal branch stays the rule's, so the utility decides what a terminal transition is worth while the rule decides how a non-terminal one is bootstrapped and neither reads the other (D-19, D-20). A utility is never an argument of `learn`: accepting one there would make the record `learn` admits depend on which utility its learner carries, which is the interface difference between catalogue members that ASM-25 refuses for the rules (ASM-25, D-20).

## 4. Where the utility is consumed, and why there

The transform is applied in **`training`'s transition record** — the step that turns one hand's decisions into the batch `agents.learn` consumes — and not in `learner.py`'s terminal branch. Three reasons, in the order they bind:

- The utility needs the **hand**, not a decision: `s_before`, `s_after` and `T` are facts of a completed hand, while `agents.learn` is handed one decision and its successor with nothing that delimits an episode (ASM-25, `records.md` §4). Carrying the stack facts in the record for this one utility's benefit would change the shape every rule reads (D-19, D-20).
- The reward is the **environment's** fact before it is the learner's: ASM-12 places the hand's outcome at the seat's terminal decision and `engine.play_hand` places it, so the utility re-expresses a number the engine already computed rather than adding a second outcome (ASM-12, D-2).
- The TD bootstrap must stay the rule's business: a reward transform inside `learn` would put two axes into one arithmetic, and a curve could no longer say which of them moved it (D-19, D-20).

`agents` still **owns the identity** — the catalogue, the spec's selection, the refusal of an unknown name and the container's field — because a pool certifies what a member is, and a learner that cannot say which utility it was trained under cannot be certified whatever arithmetic produced its numbers (§7, §8; D-20). The one risk the split leaves is stated rather than hidden: a caller that builds its own transition records and hands them to `agents.learn` gets the reward it wrote, because the reward in a transition record is the caller's — and the loop is the only caller (`records.md` §4, D-13).

The two quantities the transform needs are read, not assumed: `T` is `sum(readout["stacks"]) + readout["pot"]` at the terminal decision, and `s_0` is `engine.game_config()["starting_stack"]`, the stack the fixed game deals every hand from (D-2, ASM-6). A hand whose two totals disagree is refused by name with the hand's seed and both totals, because a normaliser that is not the game's own would make one reward incommensurable with the next (ASM-28).

## 5. The declared default, and where a utility is named

A learner spec that names no `utility` is built under the catalogue's **first** member, `net-chips`, and so is a variant entry that names none; that default is declared here and in `records.md` §2, §3 and is never inferred from the caller. Two of the five places a utility is named may be silent, because the default speaks for them — a variant entry's `utility` (`records.md` §2) and the learner spec (`records.md` §3) — and the other three **must** carry it: the checkpoint's `utility` field (§6), a pool member's `utility` (`records.md` §7), and the attribute a policy built from a learner carries (`e2e-surface.md` §2). A version record that names none is refused by name rather than read as the default, because a pool must be able to certify what its member was trained under and the container is the only witness (`records.md` §7, ASM-27). A name outside the catalogue is refused where it is declared, by name, and never mapped onto the default (D-20, D-19).

## 6. What a checkpoint records, and what a restore checks

A checkpoint records the utility its learner was trained under, in a **required** `utility` field, and `agents.load` restores the learner under it. The container's `format_version` is **3**. A **version-2** container carries a `learning_rule` and no `utility`, because it was written before the utility existed, and is read as `net-chips`; so is a **version-1** container, which carries neither; both load unchanged, and the 18 containers the repository ships are version 1 and are not regenerated (D-20, D-19, ASM-5, `protocol.md` §5). A container of either older version that *carries* the field is refused by name, because no writer of that version wrote one. A `load` spec carrying a `utility` is compared with the file's and a disagreement is refused naming both, as a spec's `learning_rule` and `seed` are; a name the catalogue does not carry is refused naming it. `evaluation.policy_from` needs nothing new — playback is utility-free — and the utility it reports is the one the file records (ASM-20).

## 7. Several utilities in one configuration, and what the records say

A configuration may declare its variants under **different** utilities, and every variant it declares is trained: its own curves, snapshots, final checkpoint, timings, record entry and pool membership. A pair whose two arms declare two utilities differs on the `utility` axis. The records that name a utility are the run record, per variant and as the run's `utilities` set (`records.md` §12); a pool record, per version member and as its `utilities` set, which the pool id digests (`records.md` §7, §8; ASM-27); a cell record, per member (`records.md` §9); a matrix record, per member and as its `utility_population` (`records.md` §10); the report's pool family, completed to the population its table measured (`records.md` §13); and the shipped checkpoint index, per artefact (`protocol.md` §5). A policy returned by `agents.as_policy` or `evaluation.policy_from` carries `utility` beside `learning_rule`, so a table names each member's objective even when a variant was built in process and no record of it exists yet (`e2e-surface.md` §2, D-20).

## 8. The fairness rule

`utility` joins the axes beside `backend`, `encoding`, `architecture`, `connectivity`, `residual` and `rule`: the axis token `fairness` reports in `axis["of"]` is **`utility`**, and the field a differing pair lists in `axis["differs"]` is **`utility`** too — one word, unlike the rule's own pairing of the token `rule` with the field `learning_rule`. What a rung holds constant is the **set** of utilities its variants declare, as it holds the set of rules: every variant entry of the six rung declarations names `net-chips` in `training.rungs()`, so no rung quietly asks its two arms different questions, and a report states the set it read (`ladder.md` §3, §4, ASM-7, D-19, D-20). A pair differs on **one** axis — two arms that declare two utilities *and* differ on `backend`, `encoding`, `architecture`, `connectivity` or `residual` are refused by name, both axes reported — and two arms that differ on `utility` alone are the comparison §10 makes (D-3, D-6).

## 9. What a table may not be read as

The league measures the game in the game's own currency: hands won, ties, losses and chips per hand, per cell, in both directions (`league.md` §4, `protocol.md` §1). A member trained on `stack-shortfall` is judged in chips, and that is deliberate, because chips are what the game pays and the objective a model was trained under is not a defence against losing them (D-20). The two are not like-for-like, so **the report and the table name each member's utility** — the matrix's `utility_population` beside its `population`, each cell's `utilities` beside its `rules` — or the ladder would silently compare models that were asked different questions (D-20, D-6, `league.md` §8).

**A differently-motivated model is neither better nor worse by the table alone.** A lower chip delta from a risk-averse model is the preference D-20 chose, not a defect D-20 introduced, and a higher one is no evidence that the objective it replaced was wrong; the table can still say what it always said — this member won more of these hands than that one at this cell size — and nothing about which objective a reader ought to have chosen (D-20, `league.md` §8, ASM-29).

## 10. The verification D-20 asks for

Two variants that differ **only** in `utility`, at one backend, one encoding, one architecture, one rule and one `hyper`, on the fixed seeds `0, 1, 2`, at the live budget of 1,000 hands per variant per seed (`protocol.md` §2, ASM-8). The question is the one the change exists to answer: **does a risk-averse objective prefer `always-call`'s ±2 to raising's ±20, and does the raise share therefore fall?** The action census is `strata/records/r2-plateau-diagnosis.md`'s method — one match per member, seats alternating, the member's own decisions counted by descriptor — and the chips per hand are that same match's payoffs, reported against `always-call` and against the other variant's model at the same seed, with a standard-error interval on every mean (D-6, D-16, ASM-23).

Measured 2026-11-09 on CPU inside the sandbox (D-8), from one `training.run` of the two-variant configuration and one census per checkpoint. The numbers are this measurement's own generation and a re-run produces its own, so they are stated as this section's measurement and never quoted elsewhere (`protocol.md` §2, ASM-23); the command that produced them is the one below.

```text
PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/python notebooks/utility-ablation.py
```

**Both arms trained.** Two variants, `net-chips-arm` and `stack-shortfall-arm`, one backend (`numpy`), one encoding (`flat-numeric`), one architecture (`naive-stack`, width 36, depth 2), one rule (`masked-q-learning`), one declared `hyper`, seeds `0, 1, 2`, 1,000 hands each — trained in one `training.run` call against one frozen pool. The run record's `utilities` set holds both names, and each variant's entry names its own. The six final checkpoints are six distinct files under two variant directories, and the two arms' **parameters differ**: at every seed about 82 % of the container's 20,238 values differ (largest absolute difference 0.2145–0.3900) while `param_count` is 10,119 for both, and the only spec field of the `metadata.json` that moved is `utility` — `learning_rule`, `format_version` (3), `kind`, `settings`, `representation.size` and `seed` are equal. The two arms' learning curves differ too on the same hands and the same initial draw, which the battery asserts (FAM-18.7). One capacity, two models, two objectives (`utilities.md` §3, ASM-10, D-20).

| Variant | Utility | Raise | Check-call | Fold | Chips/hand vs `always-call` (pooled, per seed) | Chips/hand vs the other variant |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `net-chips-arm` | `net-chips` | 93.37 % | 6.47 % | 0.16 % | −0.014 (−0.276 … +0.131; every per-seed interval spans 0) | +0.378, +0.347, +0.723 per seed |
| `stack-shortfall-arm` | `stack-shortfall` | 63.63 % | 33.87 % | 2.50 % | −0.075 (−0.341 … +0.068; every per-seed interval spans 0) | the negation of the above |

The census counted the two arms' own decisions, not their opponents': 11,943 for `net-chips-arm` and 11,163 for `stack-shortfall-arm` over the six 1,000-hand matches. Raise share by seed: `net-chips-arm` 97.29 / 95.91 / 86.87 %, `stack-shortfall-arm` 60.55 / 48.28 / 82.36 % — the two sets do not overlap, and the worst `net-chips` seed raises 4.5 points more than the best `stack-shortfall` seed.

**The verdict on the question this change exists to answer: yes, the risk-averse objective lowered the raise share, and it did so by a margin that clears the seed spread.** The pooled raise share falls from 93.4 % to 63.6 % — 29.7 points — with the mass moving to check-call (6.5 % → 33.9 %) and, slightly, to fold (0.2 % → 2.5 %), which is exactly the preference D-20 predicted from its arithmetic: a ±20-chip hand scores +1.000 or −3.000 under `stack-shortfall` and so is worth −1.0 in expectation under the measured coin flip, while a ±2-chip hand is worth −0.01, so the cheaper action is the one the objective rewards (`strata/records/r2-plateau-diagnosis.md`, D-20). The behaviour changed; the game did not.

**And the chips do not follow — which is the second half of the answer, and the half a reader must not skip.** Against `always-call`, both arms sit within a tenth of a chip per hand of zero and every per-seed interval spans zero: the risk-averse model is **−0.061 chips/hand** against the chip-optimising one, which at 1,000 hands per seed is not a resolvable difference. Head-to-head the `net-chips` model took the larger share at all three seeds (+0.756, +0.694, +1.446 chips/hand, differences whose per-seed 95 % intervals all include zero), and no single seed resolves it; combining the three seeds' 3,000 hands — three independent batches, so the mean's standard error is the seeds' standard errors combined in quadrature and divided by three — the difference is **+0.97 ± 0.42 chips/hand**, whose interval excludes zero. So at that pooled size the chip-optimising model does beat the risk-averse one, and the census shows the mechanism: the shortfall arm folds 2.3 points more often, and a fold hands the pot to a raiser. That is a real cost of the preference, measured, not a defect of the mechanism — and it is why §9 says a differently-motivated model is neither better nor worse by a table read alone (D-20, ASM-29).
