# PyPoker-Demo — L1 Phase 1 design

- **Layer:** L1 · **Unit:** phase1 · **Rev:** 20 · **Date:** 2026-11-15
- **Scope:** the technique ladder on one fixed game (brief §2) and its league evaluation (brief §3, D-16), whose learning rules, reward utilities and fixed baselines are parameters of it (D-19, D-20, D-21), whose game is now three declared shapes rather than one (D-29, D-33), and whose behaviour is read through a declared loaded deal as well as through a census (D-70)
- **Ceiling:** 56 lines. Raised from 51 at Rev 20 for the two rows and the one paragraph that index the two owners D-70 adds, for the reason the Rev 19 raise gives: this is the index of owners, so splitting it would duplicate the list it exists to be (D-70, D-61, D-65)

## What changed at Rev 2 … Rev 19

Rev 2 answered D-16: a bounded round-robin league — the model's last M versions plus the three fixed baselines — became both the training and the evaluation opposition, `league.md` owns the pool, the scheduler, the matrix, the rating and the tables, and `e2e-surface.md` replaced `evaluation.head_to_head` with the league's entry points, 11 `evaluation` endpoints and 49 across the map. Rev 3–Rev 9 repaired the blind walkthroughs of D-14, and each repair is carried by the document that owns its question: the matrix is separated from the rating and a rank asserts no head-to-head win (`league.md` §3.1, §8), a measured per-hand band replaced ASM-15's 40 ms bound (`league.md` §7, ASM-15, ASM-21), `battery.md` §2 gives the supported way to run the batteries and where they write, `ladder.md` §7 says which notebook ships and that it finds the repository root and its own write root, `getting-started.md` is the newcomer route, `records.md` §9 states what a recorded cell seed reproduces, `protocol.md` §4 records that reproducibility is one build's and one pinned interpreter's, `gpu.md` carries D-17's measurement that a delegated agent cannot reach the GPU, and ASM-20 states that a checkpoint records the seed and the encoding it was built at. Rev 10 added ASM-23: a document states a result's **shape** and the **band** it lives in and points at the artefact for the number, so no prose figure outlives its build (`protocol.md` §2).

Rev 11–Rev 13 answered D-19 and D-20, and together they made the two things a learner is built from into catalogues. `rules.md` owns the learning-rule catalogue (`masked-q-learning`, `double-q-learning`), `utilities.md` owns the terminal-reward catalogue (`net-chips`, `stack-shortfall`), every variant a configuration declares is **trained**, and a pool member, a cell, a matrix, a run record and a report each name the rule and the utility they measured, with the pool id digesting both (D-19, D-20, ASM-25–ASM-29). Rev 14 answers D-21: the baseline catalogue grows from three to six — the three of D-6 that anchor the rating and three simple folding baselines — and `folders.md` owns them: their exact rules, the `hand_class` fact they need and what carrying it costs, the anchor that does not move, what the full declared set does to K and to the tables' cost, and the live-budget verification the change exists for (D-21, ASM-30–ASM-33).

Rev 15 answers D-29, and it is a **design and not a build**: the sized-action artefact path for a second game shape. `shapes.md` owns the shape declaration — one new entry point, `engine.game_shapes()`, eight rows widened by an optional argument whose absence is today's value, and the four carriers a shape reaches — together with what stays fixed-limit evidence and the four supersessions D-2, D-3, D-4 and ASM-6 require by name. `sizing.md` owns the sized action itself: four raise bands over the legal interval and the attribution that makes a recorded amount belong to one descriptor, an index order that is the measured price order so the existing tie-break cannot read as a preference, a 1,000-hand greedy pass with exploration off as the only decision stream a run retains, the refused history size bucket and the cheaper alternative, the head as the shape's and the container's version 4, and the acceptance criterion pinned before any run — five seeds, judged per seed, with chips reported and never thresholded (D-29, `assumptions.md` ASM-35–ASM-41). The 18 checkpoints, `checkpoints/evidence/r1-live.json`, `tables/league-full.json`, the six rungs and both deep runs are untouched and are never compared across shapes (`shapes.md` §5–§6).

Rev 16 answers D-33, and it is the first change since D-29 that moves the observation's own width: D-32 recorded that a no-limit pool cannot seat a trained raiser because one 24-slot history block was both shapes', so `shapes.md` §8 makes the block's capacity the shape's — 24, 66 and 98, each derived from that game's own betting — and states what the widening costs in width, head, `param_count`, container and greedy-pass bound, while `seats.md` declares the third shape (six seats, no-limit, 100-chip stacks), the five-opponent draw without replacement, the rotating learner seat, the four refusals the pairwise instrument now makes, the unchanged 50-endpoint map, and the five-seed census-and-chips test the shape exists for (D-33, D-32, `assumptions.md` ASM-42–ASM-44). Rev 17 answers D-43, which **overrides D-41's refusal** and authorises the surface change D-41 said was required: `counterfactual.md` states the episode boundary the transition record gains and the reveal the episode record gains (with their bounds, their record cost and the readout they leave untouched), chooses the **reveal** over the replay with the cost of that choice, fixes the counterfactual's arithmetic as one whole-hand net-chip delta and proves it reproduces the realised delta at the action taken, values **every** legal action with the continuation assumption a raise declares, places the member on a **third catalogue** beside the rule and the utility with its declared step count — which corrects `rules.md` §2's "a rule swap moves no budget" — and pins the two-arm, two-seed criterion before any run (D-43, `rules.md` §2, `records.md` §4, §5, §8, §12, `battery.md` §3 FAM-22).

Rev 18 answers D-61 and D-62 and it is the change that alters what an input **means**. `normalisation.md` owns it: every value both encodings emit lies in [0, 1] — the dense layout slot by slot with its declared numerator, denominator and saturation rule, the token path through a bounded embedding, because an id is a label and its only magnitude is the row the trunk reads — a readout that cannot be normalised is refused by name, the invariant is asserted by a new battery family (FAM-24) plus the features and agents batteries rather than by any existing case being weakened, the layouts keep their widths while their values change so the shipped 18 containers become read-only history and the byte-identical gate is superseded for this change only, and the twelve-cell mechanism grid of D-62 — utility × estimator × encoding at six seats, 15,000 hands × seeds 0–2, the mixed pool, concurrent single-threaded processes — is declared with its four instruments (census, chips, active units, distinct score vectors) and its alive-or-unusable column (D-61, D-62, D-60, D-59).

Rev 19 answers D-65, and it is the last experiment D-63's cause admits. `reward-scale.md` owns it: a third utility member, `net-chips-normalised`, whose terminal reward is the seat's net chip delta divided by the game's chips in play, so the reward is bounded by 1 and the declared learning rate stops exploding the weights — the normalised reward the trunk-death RCA names, with the alternatives it rejects (clamping the raw reward, which changes the objective rather than its scale, and lowering the rate, which D-65 measured as stalling), the cost it does not have (no encoder, layout, container or endpoint moves, and the existing two members are proved untouched on a captured baseline), the rule that a record's chips stay **raw** so a normalised-reward model is readable beside every recorded cell, and the six-cell test — `net-chips-normalised` across the estimator and encoding axes at six seats, 15,000 hands × seeds 0–2, the mixed pool, concurrent single-threaded processes — with its prediction pinned before the run and its verdict stated either way (D-65, D-20, D-60).

Rev 20 answers D-70, and it is the first instrument this layer has designed that reads behaviour **one hand at a time** rather than as a share of thousands. `loaded-deal.md` owns the capability — one optional `deal` argument on `new_hand` and `play_hand`, a deal of hole cards per seat and a three-to-five-card board in one card spelling, six named refusals that precede the deal, the episode record's `deal` key with its if-and-only-if, the determinism the capability promises, and the four proof obligations a build is held to, the seeded path's byte-identical capture first among them — and `scenarios.md` owns the suite: sixteen declared scenarios (eight hole sets, each on a board that helps it and on one dry board that honestly serves all eight negative cases), four folders and a caller in the other five seats, a big-blind seating that makes both reported decisions fold-free and an opening seating where folding is offered, eighteen post-D61-era containers and the era rule that admits only them, and the binary answer rule — does the action depend on the cards at all (D-70, D-69, D-59).
